## 0. How to read this document

Every requirement has an ID, a **shall** statement, the defect or design clause it comes from, the code location where the change lands (file:line as verified on 12 Sep 2026; treat lines as approximate after any edit), an acceptance test the tester can run without asking the implementer what was meant, and the build stage from design §19. Priority is **M** (must — the stage cannot close without it), **S** (should — closes in the same stage unless blocked, with the blocker recorded) or **C** (could — deferred without penalty).

Terms carry the design's meaning (§4). Four words are used precisely throughout: **thesis** is the ticker-level direction hypothesis; **contract** is one exact OCC symbol; **assessment** is one contract at one evidence cutoff; **fill** is a broker-confirmed execution and nothing else.

Two rules the developer must internalise before reading further, because most historical regressions in this codebase came from violating one of them:

1. **No governed opportunity is lost.** Every structurally valid ticker thesis remains represented as active, monitoring, historical or a named integrity exception. Raw duplicates and superseded physical rows need not appear in every downstream or trader-facing population, but their lineage remains in canonical history. A failed contract or quote check changes the relevant contract/execution state; it never deletes the thesis. Every population transition reconciles at its own entity grain.
2. **One producer per fact.** Every field has exactly one writer. If a requirement needs a value another context owns, it consumes that context's contract; it never recomputes.

### 0.1 What these requirements deliver, and what they do not

They make the pipeline **truthful and measurable**: every thesis retained, every contract valued at a stated scenario with stated assumptions, every quote carrying its own timestamp, every decision and fill recorded, every probability labelled calibrated or not. They remove the mechanisms that were destroying edge before it could be observed — unreachable targets, wrong-unit spreads, stale quotes labelled executable, pooled direction-blind base rates, macro joins on empty sectors, contract families never valued.

They do **not** create edge. Whether the thesis engine's P(move) beats the cost of expressing it in long options is an empirical result that WP4 (calibration on stored history) will answer on data the desk already holds, before capital depends on it. A developer who reads a requirement as "make the number of trades go up" has misread it; the only population metric is the reconciliation in §8.

## 1. Scope

**In scope.** Long single-leg CALL and PUT opportunities over governed 1–20 session holds; the Evening (completed-session) run, the pre-open thesis check, the post-open contract refresh; DOI family valuation and ranking; macro advisory context; the Intelligence Lab read model and the coaching projections generated from it; the decision/fill/outcome ledger; replay and release evidence.

**Out of scope.** Any automated order placement; any automated position sizing (affordability is displayed, never applied); spreads or multi-leg structures; changes to Discovery's structural logic beyond carrying identity fields; Worker 3 behavioural engine internals; and changes to the GEX proxy's analytical model. The `build_macro_json.py` packet archival, hashing and replay-resolution boundary required by REQ-WP0-06 is explicitly in scope; its macro calculations otherwise remain unchanged.

## 13. Glossary (developer subset)

**Vol budget** — the 1σ cumulative expected move for the governed hold. **Reachable target** — spot ± k × vol budget; a scenario boundary. **Reach ratio** — structural distance ÷ vol budget; > 1.5 means the structural target is beyond one comfortable budget. **Time stop** — end of the governed hold in XNYS sessions. **Scenario payoff** — option value at the scenario spot at the time stop, remaining calendar time to expiry, constant IV. **Deterministic utility** — DOI's uncalibrated ranking score; a relative ordering, not a probability. **Calibrated p** — P(target before invalidation within hold) from stored history, with lineage. **Provider timestamp** — when the provider observed the quote; the only timestamp that determines freshness. **Fill** — a broker-confirmed execution. **Counterfactual** — what a presented-but-not-taken candidate did afterwards.
