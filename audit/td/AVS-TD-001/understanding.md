# Understanding — AVS-DISC-002 (discovery half)

This document reaches no verdict and closes no item. Closure authority rests with the acceptance half of this exercise and with the fix registers.

No NORMAL_COMPLETED_SESSION run exists. Every measurement below is RESEARCH_ONLY. Outcomes are reconstructed from daily prices on candidates the pipeline presented but nobody traded; there are no fills and no resolved ledger outcomes. Stored books were read for structure only. Finding numbers refer to `findings.md`; premise notes to `premise_notes.md`.

## 1. Where did the money go, and did we see it coming? (M1)

There was plenty of money moving between stocks. Across the pipeline's universe from 2024 to September 2026, the typical session's spread of 5-session returns was a standard deviation of 6.8%, with 9.6% at 10 sessions and 13.7% at 20 (DISC-F01). Most of that spread sits inside sectors rather than between them: the typical sector's own spread is about 0.86 of the whole universe's (DISC-F02).

Whether the pipeline saw it coming is not measurable on current data. The four USMI routing labels exist only in packets dated 11 September, and the price store ends on 10 September, so no label has a single later price to be scored against (DISC-F05). The stored macro-to-ticker join adds nothing either way: every row of the primary book reads NEUTRAL with reason `SECTOR_UNMAPPED`, although every row has a sector (DISC-F04). Sector relative strength over one week carries essentially no information about the next 5 to 20 sessions (autocorrelation −0.04 to +0.01; DISC-F03), so a label describing last week's sector strength would need to know something beyond momentum to lead the move. Answering this would need dated routing packets archived by ID across at least 17–100 sessions per label, with forward prices after each.

## 2. Is there a move the market has not priced, and do we know something about it? (M2)

The forecast runs high against what actually happened. Realised volatility came in at a median 70–80% of the forecast, and the one-sigma band captured about 80% of moves where 68% would be expected (DISC-F08). The forecast also sits above implied volatility, but by 8–25% at the median depending on the IV source, not the 20–40% the design cites (DISC-F09; PN-47). Implied volatility itself sat 43–87% above realised moves over the same seven weeks, so both the market and the forecast priced more movement than arrived.

The headline test asked whether the forecast adds information once implied volatility is known. With weekly surface IV, the forecast's coefficient is positive and statistically significant at every horizon (b_F 0.19 / 0.31 / 0.20 at 5 / 10 / 20 sessions; heteroskedasticity-robust t 3.8 / 6.2 / 4.7; session-clustered t 2.7 / 9.6 / 7.7). It holds for CALL rows at every horizon and for PUT rows at none (DISC-F06). The result does not survive better IV measurement: averaging IV sources drops the 5-session t to 1.6, and chain-based IV at 20 sessions drops it to 0.25 (PN-46). The weekly IV is up to five sessions stale and one tenor bucket serves both 10 and 20 sessions, and IV measurement error pushes explanatory weight onto the correlated forecast. So the data are consistent with some incremental information on CALL rows, but cannot separate it from stale IV. It is not established that the forecast knows something the market does not. A per-state bias correction does no better than one overall correction, and neither brings the ratio into the validation band (DISC-F11). Settling the question would need daily ATM IV at 7, 14 and 28 days to expiry, captured at every forecast session.

## 3. When the thesis was right, what stopped it monetising? (M3)

Direction was roughly a coin toss; the size of the move was the binding constraint. On 8,417 theses, the close ended on the thesis side 45% of the time for CALLs and 54% for PUTs, but the volatility-budget target was reached first on only 7% and 3% (DISC-F14). The median favourable excursion was about 4%, against a median target distance of about 14% (PN-40).

Only 15% of theses could be priced against stored option history, at roughly one snapshot each, so monetisation counts are a lower bound. Among priced theses that were on the right side, 78% never showed a bid of 1.25 times the entry ask (DISC-F15). The most common first cause was a favourable move smaller than the strike distance (27% CALL, 31% PUT), then flat-scenario decay of 25% or more (25%, 28%), which appeared as one of several causes on about 70% (DISC-F16). Where the thesis was wrong, the invalidation level was touched on 72% of CALLs, and by the end of the hold the loss exceeded the stop distance on 62%, with a median 1.24 times the stop; the stop sits nearly five times closer to spot than the target (DISC-F17; PN-39). The binding constraint is therefore expression — a target sized at 1.5 budgets against moves that typically travel a third of that, bought through contracts that decay while waiting — rather than direction. Daily option-chain snapshots through each hold would turn the lower bound into a measurement.

## 4. What is the cheapest way to express a correct thesis, and how far is the pipeline from it? (M4)

For CALLs, reachable-target payoff per dollar peaks at a delta of about 0.34–0.36, further out of the money than the legacy 0.40–0.60 selection band. The 0.25–0.40 band beats 0.40–0.60 on two thirds of 10-session CALL theses, and the 0.60–0.85 band beats it on only one in ten (DISC-F22). Within the thesis family, the pipeline's selected CALL delivers a median 88% of the best available payoff, and where it misses it mostly sits at a higher delta than the best contract (DISC-F24). Widening the delta band from 0.40–0.60 to 0.10–0.85 raises the median number of candidates per thesis from 2 to 7 and cuts theses with no candidate from 169 to 39 (DISC-F21).

The breakeven move measured in forecast sigmas has a median of 0.75 for CALLs. CALL theses whose contracts needed the smaller move closed on the right side 62% of the time against 48% for the rest, the only association in the track that survives correction for multiple testing (DISC-F27). That may reflect volatility conditions rather than contract choice, because a low ratio co-varies with the forecast sitting above IV. Pricing at the structural target instead of the reachable target cuts the median payoff to 0.68 of the reachable figure and changes which contract looks best on 46% of theses (DISC-F28). These answers rest on 929 theses from four decision sessions, because chain snapshots exist for only five dates; every PUT cell is underpowered.

## 5. What does waiting cost, and what does confirmation buy? (M5)

Holding the same contract costs time decay first: about 2%, 7%, 17% and 26% of premium after 1, 2, 3 and 5 sessions, while the implied-volatility component moves premium by between −0.2% and +8% (DISC-F35; PN-41). The early-lane figure — the median monetisable row buys IV 1.9% lower than earlier — reproduces exactly on 148 rows, but its lookback is four trading sessions not three, its interquartile range is 12 percentage points, and on the 10 September books the sign flips to IV 3–4% higher (DISC-F29–F31). A median that small inside a spread that wide is not a stable decision input (PN-38).

Confirmation buys nothing measurable. Where cells have at least 100 rows, the change in the probability of reaching the target between one session and a later one has a median of zero at every delay (DISC-F36), but base rates of 2–7% leave the standard error the same size as every measured change (PN-56). The 453 stale contracts on 11 September have no later stored quote, so their own move is unmeasurable; comparable contracts in the same book moved a median 12% in mid price over the day, and the next day's ask sat 30% above the prior mid (DISC-F32, F33). The waiting inequality in §4.6 appears sign-inverted as written, so its evaluation is recorded as a premise note and not as a result (PN-13).

## 6. What are the gates selecting on, and what is on the other side? (M6)

The gates are not consistently removing losers. The Options Intelligence scope removed CALL rows that went on to hit the target 11.4% of the time, against 8.1% for the rows it kept, in 9 of 12 runs (DISC-F37). The pre-book EIL and EOD stage removed rows that did better on both directions (DISC-F38), and the spread gate removed PUT rows that hit 8.9% against 3.3% for survivors while doing the opposite for CALLs (DISC-F39). Two gates kept the better CALL rows: the OI stand-down label (11.1% against 6.9%) and the BLOCKED Lab verdict (8.1% against 3.7%) (DISC-F40, F41). The comparisons use a target that widens with volatility, and several gates select on volatility, so each gap mixes what the gate chooses with how far away its target sits (PN-54).

What the gates select on is mostly the pipeline's own priority and options scores, not its expected-value measures. Whether a row reaches the GO set is almost unrelated to any score (correlation at most 0.16); the non-blocked set tracks `priority_score` at 0.68, and book rank follows `priority_score` by construction (DISC-F42, F43). The macro route removed nothing because it never took a value other than NEUTRAL (DISC-F46). Stocks move together enough that the effective number of independent bets stays at 11–15 whether a gate leaves 1,572 rows or 994 (DISC-F44). Every gate added from 31 August onward is underpowered, because its outcome windows have not elapsed.

## 7. What is EV currently made of? (M7)

As wired, production values nothing: the DOI valuation receives no forecast volatility, and every contract comes out not evaluated. With the forecast supplied by the tester, EV is carried by the assumption that a 1.5-sigma favourable move is reached, not by the forecast sitting above implied volatility. The forecast-over-IV share of EV has a median of −0.01 for CALLs and 0.17 for PUTs, and re-pricing with the forecast set equal to IV keeps 95% of positive CALL contracts and 98% of positive PUT contracts positive (DISC-F48, F49; PN-02).

The sign of EV is set by the drift assumption. Weighting payoffs by double-barrier probabilities with the thesis drift turns 40% of positive CALL contracts and 75% of positive PUT contracts non-positive; with zero drift, positive CALL contracts fall from 689 to 115 and PUT from 360 to 8 on the structural barrier, and to 15 and 7 on the reachable barrier (DISC-F51; PN-01 — the largest premise note). EV rank falls as delta and premium rise, so the ranking favours cheap, low-delta leverage (DISC-F50). Friction as modelled is decided by the 0.30 spread limit, which removes 896 contracts, while the 0.15 cap never binds; with no fills, a full half-spread is the only supportable assumption (DISC-F53, F55; PN-08).

## 8. Under what conditions do outcomes beat the base rate, with what confidence? (M8)

Of 970 tests across 14 conditioning variables, 248 had at least 100 theses; 48 survive false-discovery correction both pooled and within decision sessions (DISC-F56, F58). The largest differences are by sector, mostly in whether the close ended on the right side: for 6–10 session CALLs, Utilities 28 points below the base rate, Real Estate 24 below, Energy 14 above; for 6–10 session PUTs, Consumer Discretionary 23 above, with the sign holding in 8–10 of 10 sessions (DISC-F59). These describe one July–September market path with overlapping windows and recurring tickers, not a forward sector signal, and the surviving p-values remain optimistic by an unmeasured factor.

Target-first differences that survive are 2–14 points. Regime labels take one value per run and cannot be separated from the session, including the largest pooled gap in the track (DISC-F61). Compression and hidden state push CALL and PUT in opposite directions, which fits a shared drift in those names rather than directional information (DISC-F62). Base rates vary more across sessions than most conditions move them within sessions (DISC-F57; PN-21). Delta band, days to expiry, catalyst presence and phase show nothing, largely because those fields are empty or defaulted on most books (DISC-F65). Detecting a 5-point difference at 80% power needs 131–229 theses per cell for target-first and about 780 for the right-side measure at nominal significance, rising to about 2,050 at family-wise significance (DISC-F66).

## 9. What is the minimum data set for current decisions, and for the measurements above? (M9)

Current decisions use a small part of what is written. The final-authority readers branch on 34 fields (19 of them in the Lab book); the six named decision readers read 93; the broadest rule reaches 243 (DISC-F67, F75). The Lab book carries 502 fields, of which 200 are constant on the run and 62 are entirely empty (DISC-F68, F70). Direction, contract symbol, ask, quote timestamp and target each appear under four to seven identical column names (DISC-F69).

The measurements above need data no run artefact carries: forward and realised prices over the hold, realised volatility over the hold, the horizon sigma, family-wide per-contract quotes and greeks, and the thesis origin spot (DISC-F73). They also need a per-row decision session with full fill, a stable thesis identity across runs (87% of thesis IDs embed their own session date; DISC-F34), resolved units on implied volatility, expected move and spread (DISC-F71), and fills. The gap between the two sets is the instrumentation plan in `unavailable_inventory.md`.

## What we still cannot answer, and precisely what would be needed

- **Whether routing labels lead sector moves:** needs dated four-label USMI packets archived by `packet_id`, with forward prices after each, over roughly 17–100 packet sessions per label value.
- **Whether the forecast knows something the market does not:** needs daily ATM implied volatility per ticker at 7, 14 and 28 days to expiry, captured at every forecast session.
- **How often correct theses actually monetised, and why not:** needs daily option-chain snapshots with greeks and two-sided quotes for every contract in each thesis family through the hold, plus a same-day re-quote of stale contracts.
- **What friction really costs:** needs recorded fills with symbol, price, quantity, side and timestamp.
- **What waiting and confirmation are worth on current theses:** needs a point-in-time decision record in the ledger (completed session, integer hold, volatility budget and k, invalidation, preferred assessment ID) and matured outcome events for every presented candidate.
- **Whether any condition beats the base rate out of sample:** needs at least 134 decision sessions of normal completed-session runs for a time-ordered partition with a 20-session embargo — about 112 more than exist — and per-cell samples of roughly 131–229 theses for target-first differences, or about 2,050 for the right-side measure at family-wise significance. The 11–20 session horizon, with 58 reconstructable rows in 22 runs, will not reach this at the current flow.
- **Whether any of the above holds on the remediated code:** needs one normal completed-session Evening run, one pre-open check and one post-open refresh produced by the current commit.
