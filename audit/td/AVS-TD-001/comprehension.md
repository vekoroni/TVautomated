Written before any acceptance check ran. Items 1–6 are from the governed documents (SD v1.2, REQ v1.2, Annex A v1.2 under `docs/`). Items 7–13 are from source, with file:line evidence gathered by read-only search on commit `cc509cb`. `UNKNOWN` is used where the source did not answer.

## From the documents

**1. Reachable target vs structural target.** The structural target is the level Discovery derives from structure (asymmetry gate, Wyckoff, or a 3R fallback) and it is owned by the Thesis context; nobody downstream may change it. The reachable target is a different object: it is the origin spot moved by k times the cumulative one-sigma volatility budget for the governed hold (k = 1.5 initially). It is a scenario boundary, not a prediction. The two differ because the structural target says where structure points, while the reachable target says how far the underlying can plausibly travel inside the hold given forecast volatility. The verification found the Friday GO rows had structural targets a median 4.1 budgets away, so pricing a contract at the structural target rewards exactly the contracts whose target is least reachable. The design therefore values the contract at the reachable target and keeps the structural payoff as disclosure only (`reach_ratio = structural distance / budget` tells the trader how far apart the two are).

**2. Why macro may not size.** The design makes AVSHUNTER capital-agnostic by owner decision (2026-09-12): no account balance, desk budget, affordability class, position size or contract count may be consumed by ranking, thesis validation, execution evidence or the Lab read model (ALG-13 retired, REQ-WP5-04, NFR-02). Macro is an advisory context that can describe alignment and scenarios; if it could apply a size multiplier it would become an authority over capital, which the invariants reserve for the human. A macro-derived multiplier would also let a narrative packet silently change the economic outcome of a thesis without any change in the thesis or the contract, breaking the rule that direction, population, contract identity and capital are each owned by exactly one context.

**3. Why a decision is not a fill.** The ledger separates what the system presented, what the human decided (TAKEN / NOT_TAKEN / DEFERRED) and what actually executed. Only a broker-confirmed or manually confirmed fill record (symbol, price, quantity, side, timestamp, source) creates a position episode (invariant 10, NFR-06, REQ-WP7-01). A GO, BUY, PROBE or "decided" state is a system or human intention; it may never have reached the market, may have filled at a different price, or may have been partially filled. Counting decisions as trades would fabricate realised performance and contaminate calibration with outcomes that were never held.

**4. Why `_utc_now()` on a quote timestamp is dangerous.** Freshness is decided from the provider's observation time. If a missing provider timestamp is replaced by the fetch time or the current wall clock, a quote observed yesterday looks like it was observed seconds ago, so a stale or historical quote can be classified `EXECUTABLE_NOW` (this was the Friday 11 Sep wall: 453 rows judged on prior-session quotes). The design removes every such fallback: the provider timestamp, fetch timestamp and source dataset are three separate fields, and a quote with no provider timestamp is `CONTRACT_QUOTE_UNAVAILABLE` for execution classification (ALG-12, REQ-WP0-04). The tell-tale artefact is `quote_fetch_timestamp_utc == quote_provider_timestamp_utc` to the second.

**5. What `NORMAL_COMPLETED_SESSION` requires.** The run must be planned against the last completed XNYS session, on a clean tree with recorded code and config identity, not forced intra-session, and its option evidence must pass the two-grain provider-finality contract (ALG-15): per chain, historical request mode, settlement delay elapsed, official close present for the same ticker/session, session-date and timestamp coverage above governed thresholds, late-watermark coverage, and the full min/median/p95/max timestamp distribution recorded; at run level, the complete-chain fraction (candidate 0.95) and official-close fraction (candidate 0.99) must both pass. One late quote cannot prove completion, and a fixed UTC offset is prohibited. All of this is written into `run_meta_v2.provider_completeness_evidence`, with `baseline_eligible = true` only when the run is normal.

**6. Why a `TEST`-condition run cannot support a performance claim.** A forced intra-session, prior-session-anchored or dirty-tree run is not a point-in-time observation of the normal decision process: its quotes are a mixture of sessions, its evidence cutoff does not match the session it claims, its code identity is not reproducible, and the population it presents is not what the operator would have seen at the governed time. Any edge, hit-rate or EV measured on it would mix look-ahead and stale evidence. The design keeps such runs as engineering evidence (structure, schema, lineage, replay) and excludes them from calibration fitting and performance claims (SD §0 rule 2, ALG-08 point-in-time rule, OPS-02/07).

## From the code

**7. `garch_expected_move_*` fields.** Layer 3 produces one annualised forecast (`forward_realised_vol`, from a 20-step averaged HAR-RV / EGARCH / GARCH(1,1) forecast at `layer3_forward_variance.py:477`, `:298-305`, `:360-368`, `:388-394`) and then scales it by square-root-of-time; no per-horizon forecast survives. The legacy fields are in PERCENT and use calendar days converted at 5/7: `layer3_forward_variance.py:174-187` (`trading_frac = days * (5 / 7)`; `annualised_vol * sqrt(trading_frac / 252) * 100`). `l3_expected_move_1_5d` is cumulative for 5 calendar days; `l3_expected_move_6_10d` and `_11_20d` are **differenced in sigma space**: `layer3_forward_variance.py:521-524`:
```
em_1_5   = _expected_move(ann_vol, 5)
em_6_10  = _expected_move(ann_vol, 10) - _expected_move(ann_vol, 5)
em_11_20 = _expected_move(ann_vol, 20) - _expected_move(ann_vol, 10)
```
so `6_10d / 1_5d = 0.4142` on every row by construction. The `garch_expected_move_*` names are Lab aliases of these (`contracts/lab_control.py:2936-2938`, `:575-577`); no producer writes that prefix. The v2 cumulative fields `expected_move_5d/10d/20d_fraction` are produced in `domain/volatility_budget.py:62` (`move = vol * effective * sqrt(hold / 252.0)`) and `:69-77` (`horizon_convention = CUMULATIVE_1SIGMA`, `forecast_horizon_basis = SCALED_CURRENT_ANNUAL_FORECAST`), merged into the Layer 3 dict at `layer3_forward_variance.py:117-121`, `:155`. They use trading sessions directly, so v2 5d differs from legacy 1_5d by sqrt(7/5) ≈ 1.183 for the same forecast. Legacy fields are marked `l3_expected_move_legacy_deprecated = True` (`layer3_forward_variance.py:156`). Production readers of the legacy names: `contracts/lab_control.py`, `intelligence-lab/intelligence_lab.py`, `trigger_layer.py:335-345` (prefers `expected_move_10d_fraction` then falls back to legacy /100), `execution_intelligence.py:648-654`, `execution_decision_engine.py:744-755`, `eod_candidate_engine.py:1571, 2445, 2695-2696` (maps the differenced 6–10d value onto a field named `expected_move_10d`), `morning_validation.py:420-425`, `vanguard/ev3_stage0.py:372-389` (expects `l3_expected_move_5d/10d/20d`, names Layer 3 never emits). ## Gate assessment

Items 7–10 did not return UNKNOWN: the pipeline's core economics are readable from source. The material comprehension findings that shape the tracks are: (a) the differenced legacy expected-move fields are still what the Lab book and most consumers read; (b) two monetisability engines price different targets with different friction and different floors, and the one the Morning Gate reads is the structural-intrinsic one; (c) the DOI scenario engine, reachability, rate bridge, hysteresis and utility v2 exist in source but are only reachable through the DOI production path, which on the stored runs stopped at `FAMILY_NOT_VALUED_RATE_UNAVAILABLE`; (d) the macro join runs twice with the second overwriting the first, and the requirement's routing key field does not exist.
