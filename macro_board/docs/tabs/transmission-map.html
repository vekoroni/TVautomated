Transmission map

Through which macro channels has today's environment been moving each ETF: when a condition sat where it sits today, did this ETF do better or worse than usual?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

Macro news reaches prices through channels: rates, the dollar, credit, oil, market calm or stress. "Transmission" means how a change in one of those channels passes into an asset's price. This tab shows, for every ETF and every one of the board's 10 macro conditions, whether that ETF did better or worse in the past when the condition was in today's state.

It answers "why" questions. Why are international stocks soft? Which condition has been the headwind for semiconductors? Which ETFs barely care about macro at all? It does not give a lean or a signal. The ETF matrix does that, using all conditions together.

Use it after the ETF matrix, once you have a few ETFs in mind, and before the Desk card. It also explains the Response label on the ETF matrix: the cells with strong evidence here are the "headwinds" and "tailwinds" counted there.

Display onlyThe map describes recorded history. It never feeds a gate, score or rank, and it is not a forecast. Macro informs the human; it never decides.

What it does

The 10 conditions

Each session the board labels 10 macro conditions with a state, using only what was known at that day's close. FRED data (the US Federal Reserve's economic database) is lagged to the day it was published. Inputs older than their limit (6 days for daily series, 12 for weekly) become MISSING; the board never carries an old value forward or fills in a neutral one.

Seven conditions are "trend" conditions. The board takes the 20-session change, divides it by the change's own standard deviation over the trailing year (a z-score: how unusual the change is for that series), and calls it up or down only beyond ±0.5. That keeps roughly the middle third "stable". Curve level uses fixed bands. SPY trend, realised volatility and breadth reuse the outcome scorer's own definitions, so the board and the scorer agree.

The calculation behind each cell

  1. Forward return. For every past session, the ETF's return from the next session's open to the close 1, 5 or 20 sessions later.
  2. Split by state. Sessions where the condition was in today's state go in one group. Sessions where it was in any other observed state go in the other. Sessions where it was MISSING are left out.
  3. Excess. Mean return in today's state minus mean return in the other states. That is the number in the cell, in percentage points.
  4. Independent windows. Overlapping days are thinned, so each group counts only windows that do not share their future (see the ETF matrix guide).
  5. t. A Welch t-statistic: the excess divided by its standard error, using the independent windows of both groups. It says how far the difference stands out from noise. Cells with |t| below 1 are faded.

Which ETFs appear

Only ETFs with current prices and at least 500 sessions of history. On 13 Oct that was 77 of 115 at every horizon. Short-history ETFs (IJR, KWEB and others) are left off. Leveraged and volatility products stay on, because the map shows evidence, not leans.

When it updates

Every board build. The states change when the data changes; the cells change when the states change or a new session adds history.

Reading the screen

1. Title, horizon and legend

The title says what each cell is: "how each ETF's forward return differed while each condition sat in today's state". Next to the horizon buttons is the colour legend: a red-to-blue ramp with its end values. The ends move with the data: on 13 Oct they were ±0.2% at 1d, ±0.9% at 5d and ±3.2% at 20d.

2. Column headers

Ten vertical headers, one per condition, each reading "Name: STATE", for example "Curve level: FLAT". Hover a header to see what is measured. Read the headers first: they are today's macro picture in one line.

3. Group rows and ETF rows

ETFs sit under their group names in the config's order: Broad indices, Factors, SPDR sectors and so on. Each row holds ten coloured cells.

4. Cells

Look for two things. First, columns that light up in one colour across a whole group: a channel acting on that group. Second, rows with one or two strong cells: the channel that matters most for that ETF. Skip faded cells. Hover any cell for its numbers.

5. The note under the map

It repeats the definition and the key warning: these are single-condition contrasts on overlapping windows, so they show where macro has been transmitting, not stand-alone signals. The colour scale is capped at the 95th percentile.

Scenarios

Scenario: one channel lights up a whole group (international, 20d)

What you see: In the 20d map the "Realised volatility: LOW" column is red across the international rows with full colour: EWG -3.4 (t -2.94), FXI -3.4 (t -1.92), VEA -1.9 (t -2.03), EFA -1.8 (t -1.98), EWU -1.7 (t -1.93). All five fell over the last 20 sessions. Their Response on the ETF matrix: Supporting for EWG, VEA and EFA, Mixed for EWU, Rejecting relief for FXI.

What it means: In this history, calm US markets have been a period when foreign developed markets lagged their usual returns. Today's price action is in line with that for most of the group.

What a trader does: Uses this as the explanation for the weakness, not as a trade. None of the five has a measured lean on the ETF matrix, because the combined analog view did not separate enough. A trader would watch the realised volatility state: if SPY volatility moves to MID, this headwind no longer applies.

Scenario: channels pull in opposite directions (SPY, 20d)

What you see: SPY's 20d row has three full-colour cells. Breadth LOW: +1.3 (t 1.16). Realised volatility LOW: -0.9 (t -1.06). Curve level FLAT: -1.3 (t -1.16). SPY rose 2.72% over 20 sessions.

What it means: Two channels have historically hurt SPY and one helped. The ETF matrix counts them, two headwinds against one tailwind, and labels SPY "Absorbing pressure": it rose anyway.

What a trader does: Notes that the label rests on counts, not sizes: the tailwind and the larger headwind are almost the same size. A trader would not read SPY's rise as fragile from this alone. The map says there is no single dominant channel; the ETF matrix shows no clear lean. Wait for a clearer picture.

Scenario: a condition goes missing (illustrative)

What you see: For example, suppose the 10-year yield series stops updating and its last value becomes more than 6 days old. The header reads "10Y yield: MISSING" and every cell in that column shows ×.

What it means: The board refuses to guess. The yield channel is unknown today. The analog match on the ETF matrix also uses only the conditions that are observed, so it would be built on 9 conditions instead of 10.

What a trader does: Checks the condition tiles on the Overview tab for each input's observation date, and the Macro intelligence tab for the source status. Treats rate-sensitive ETFs with extra caution until the series returns. On 13 Oct nothing was missing, but the liquidity inputs were 4 sessions old (observed 7 Oct) and the yield and dollar inputs 2 sessions old.

Scenario: nothing to do (XLE, 20d)

What you see: XLE's 20d row is entirely faded: no condition reaches |t| of 1. The same is true for XLV, XLY, HACK, XBI, JETS, SHY and USO. Yet XLE closed at a 1-year high, up 5.41% in 5 sessions.

What it means: History does not show today's macro mix pushing XLE in a measurable way. Whatever drives XLE now is not visible through these 10 channels.

What a trader does: Does not invent a macro story. Judges XLE on price, on Sector rotation (it was LEADING) and on the Day story's flow reading instead. The map simply has nothing to add here.

Scenario: a column on the edge of its band (Curve level)

What you see: "Curve level: FLAT" carries some of the strongest 20d cells: SMH -4.2, SOXX -4.6, OIH -4.7, XHB -3.2, SLV -3.9, and UNG +7.3. The spread sits at +44 bp, 6 bp under the 0.5-point line that makes it STEEP. It was STEEP on 8 Oct and FLAT again on 9 Oct.

What it means: A small move in the 2-year or 10-year yield flips the whole column. The cells would then show each ETF's record in a steep curve, which may look quite different.

What a trader does: Treats this column as fragile for the next few sessions. A trader planning around the semis headwind checks the curve each morning before relying on it.

Scenario: bright colours, tiny numbers (1d)

What you see: On the 1d map the legend ends at ±0.2%, the minimum. Many cells look strongly coloured. The strongest by t is LQD under "Credit appetite: STABLE": -0.07% (t -2.95, 412 windows).

What it means: At one session, real differences are a few hundredths of a percent. They can be statistically clear yet far smaller than a bid-ask spread.

What a trader does: Uses the 1d map for context on today's channels, not for sizing a trade. A trader never treats a 1d cell as an edge after costs.

Applying it to a trade

You have SMH (semiconductors) on your list from the ETF matrix: down 7.02% in 5 sessions, up 8.49% over 20. Let's ask the map what has been pushing it.

  1. Choose 20d. The legend reads -3.2% … +3.2%.
  2. Find the row. Under "Industries & themes", SMH has one full-colour cell: Curve level FLAT, -4.2. All nine others are faded.
  3. Hover the cell. Excess -4.20%, t -1.88, 24 independent windows in state, mean while in state -0.28%. So in a flat curve SMH averaged -0.28% over the next 20 sessions; in the other states it averaged about +3.9%.
  4. Check the column. SOXX (-4.6), OIH (-4.7), XHB (-3.2) and SLV (-3.9) share the same red. This is a channel, not a quirk of one fund.
  5. Check the state. Curve level is at +44 bp, near the 0.5 line, and flipped twice in the last week.
  6. Cross-check the ETF matrix. Response 20d: Absorbing pressure (headwind: curve level; SMH still rose 8.49%). Response 5d: Supporting (fell 7.02%). Lean: No clear lean. Momentum history: after a 5d extended-down move, SMH's next 20 sessions averaged +5.39% (✓ holdout, 24 windows).
  7. Cross-check Sector rotation. The relative heatmap shows the same channel against SPY: Curve level -2.94 (t -1.91).

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Read the column headers first. They are today's macro picture.
  • Look down columns for group patterns, then across rows for each ETF's main channel.
  • Hover before believing. Check t and the windows count.
  • Check how close a state is to its band edge before building a plan on it.
  • Switch horizons. A channel that matters at 20d may be absent at 1d.
  • Use the map to explain the ETF matrix's Response label.

Common mistakes

  • Adding cells together. Conditions overlap (yields and the curve move together), so the cells do not sum.
  • Reading colour without the number. At 1d the scale saturates at 0.2%.
  • Treating a cell as a trade signal. It is a single-condition contrast.
  • Reading × as "no effect". It means unknown.
  • Forgetting that the comparison is "today's state versus the other states", not "versus zero".
  • Assuming textbook signs (for example "strong dollar is always bad for gold"). The map shows what this history measured.
