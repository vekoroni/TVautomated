Day story

Where is money going today, which measured facts fit that move, and what would a trade on it look like?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

The Day story turns the board's numbers into a story you can read in a few minutes. It answers three questions for every sector and industry ETF: is money moving in or out, what measured facts fit that move, and where would a trade start and where would it be wrong.

An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is a basket of stocks that trades like one stock. XLE holds energy companies, XLK holds technology companies, SPY holds the S&P 500. The board compares each sector ETF with SPY to see where money is rotating.

Read it second in your routine, right after the Daily review. The Daily review tells you how the market reacted to the day's news. The Day story tells you which sectors that money went into or out of. Then move on to the Overview and the Sector rotation for the deeper evidence.

Display only

Nothing on this tab is a gate, a score or a rank. It never feeds the pipeline. Macro informs a human; it never decides. The tab writes its flow calls to a ledger so the board can check later whether its own reads were right.

What it does

Three voices, each labelled

Every sentence on this tab carries one of three labels, so you always know who is speaking.

  • MEASURED: sentences built only from the board's own numbers (prices, volume, stored option chains, measured macro links). This is the voice to trust first.
  • PUBLISHED · not verified: items from the event payload. Another LLM (large language model, an AI text writer) made that payload. The board matches its items to sectors by keyword and does not check them.
  • AI NARRATIVE: a written story by Claude (model claude-opus-5-5), fed only the measured facts on this tab. The board checks every number and every flow call it writes against the facts and flags any mismatch.

Inputs

  • Prices for the 11 SPDR sector ETFs and the industry ETFs listed in the rotation config (26 ETFs on 13 Oct), plus SPY as the benchmark.
  • Share volume for each ETF over the last 21 sessions. A session is one trading day.
  • Stored option chains from MarketData.app, kept in the phantom database table iv_surface_history. The board reads the last 60 stored dates per ETF. It uses the front bucket of expiries 8 to 30 days out.
  • Macro condition sensitivities from the Transmission map: how each ETF did in past sessions when a condition sat in today's state.
  • Cross-assets: TLT (long Treasuries), UUP (US dollar), USO (oil), HYG (high-yield credit) and GLD (gold).
  • Price levels from the opportunity owner: 252-day high and low, 50-day and 200-day averages, GEX walls and flip, and the pipeline's target and invalidation.
  • Option pricing against history from the Next session plan, live scenarios from Scenario watch, and pipeline candidates from the Next session plan.
  • Published items from the event payload, and the macro packet's sector bias.

Steps, in plain words

  1. Relative return. For each ETF the board subtracts SPY's return from the ETF's return over 1, 5 and 20 sessions. The answer is in points (pts), meaning percentage points. XLE +5.55 over 5 sessions means XLE beat SPY by 5.55 percentage points.
  2. Flow label. The 5-session number sets the direction and the 20-session number says whether that is new. A 5-session move smaller than 0.25 pts either way is No clear flow. Up by 0.25 or more with the 20-session number below -0.5 is Turning in; otherwise Money in. Down by 0.25 or more with the 20-session number above +0.5 is Leaving a leader; otherwise Money out. These bands come from opportunity.relative_band_pp and are provisional.
  3. Volume. The last session's share volume divided by its average over the previous 20 sessions.
  4. Option footprint. What option traders paid for on the last stored chain, compared with the ETF's own stored history. Guards stop the board reading data that is stale, too short, unstable or captured with different coverage (details in the tables).
  5. Reasons (drivers). Macro conditions in today's state where the ETF has a measured 5-session response with |t| of 2 or more, and cross-assets the ETF's relative return has moved with (|correlation| of 0.3 or more over 60 sessions). Each reason either fits the flow, goes against it, or is neutral. At most 5 are kept.
  6. Flow record. For each flow label, what the ETF's relative return did over the next 5 sessions after the same label in all past history.
  7. Trade analysis. The side follows the flow (money in means calls, money out means puts). The trigger and the "wrong if" level are the nearest levels above and below the current price. Options are labelled cheap, fair or rich against history.
  8. Ticker impact. Each pipeline candidate is read against its sector ETF's flow.
  9. Ledger. Pre-open and evening builds write each ETF's flow call to output/day_story_ledger.jsonl (first build of a session wins). Later builds score those calls on the next 1 and 5 sessions.
  10. AI narrative. On pre-open (MORNING) and evening builds, Claude writes the story from the facts. It is cached per facts, so an identical set of facts is not sent twice. Other builds show the session's latest narrative, labelled as written for an earlier build.

Terms you will meet

Markets

  • Benchmark: the yardstick, here SPY.
  • Daily move: one normal day's move for that ETF, from its 20-session realised volatility divided by the square root of 252.
  • Implied volatility (IV): the yearly move the option price implies, in %.
  • ATM (at the money): the strike nearest the current price.
  • 25-delta skew: how much more a downside put costs than an upside call, in IV points. Rising skew means traders pay up for protection.
  • Open interest (OI): option contracts still held at the end of the day.
  • Put/call ratio: put volume divided by call volume.

Statistics

  • Correlation (corr): from -1 to +1, how closely two daily return series moved together.
  • t (t-statistic): how many standard errors an average sits from zero. |t| of 2 or more is unlikely to be pure noise.
  • Independent windows: past cases counted so that overlapping periods are not double counted.
  • Percentile: the share of past values at or below today's value.
  • Rotation quadrant: Leading, Weakening, Lagging or Improving, from the Sector rotation map.

Reading the screen

The tab runs from the whole market down to single tickers. Read it in that order.

1. The header and the three-voices box

The header names the next session ("trade analysis for 2026-10-14"). The box below it explains the three labels. Read it once; after that, just watch the labels.

2. The day ahead

This repeats the Next session plan's market call ("market call mixed" on 13 Oct), its sentences and its approach. It lists scheduled releases before the next close and the published narrative. On 13 Oct it showed CPI at 08:30 ET on 14 Oct, with the note that SPY moved 1.25x a normal day on 63 past release days. Read this first: it tells you how much of the day may be decided by one print.

3. Possible paths for the index ETFs

One card each for SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA. Each card shows the reference price and its basis (for example "PREMARKET 05:04 ET"), a normal day, what options price for the next session, and three branches: Holds the range, Breaks up, Breaks down. Under them sit the option footprint and two lines of history.

4. The story in words (AI NARRATIVE)

Check the line of check marks first. "every number found in the facts" and "flow calls match the measurement" mean the text passed verification. Then read the overview, the sector and ticker stories, the risks and what would change the view.

5. Where money is going (MEASURED)

A summary of who is in and who is out over 5 sessions, then one row per ETF. Rows with a clear flow come first, largest 5-session move first. Click a row to open its detail panel: evidence, option footprint, reasons, trade analysis, flow record, live scenarios, published items and pipeline candidates.

6. Trade analysis for the day

Up to 8 ETFs with a clear flow, each with side, trigger, "wrong if" level, option pricing, evidence for and against, the flow's own record and live scenarios. This is the shortlist. Each table has a CSV button to download it.

7. Ticker impact

Pipeline candidates read against their sector ETF's flow. The header counts how many are with the flow, against it, stock-specific, or in a sector with no clear flow.

8. The board's own flow calls

The live scorecard for this tab. It says how many past flow calls have been scored and whether money kept going the way the board said.

What it means: The measured pieces agree on the put side, and the trigger is about one normal day away. Two things hold it back: light volume and no measured habit of follow-through.

Scenario: stale or unreadable option data (XLP and XLRE, 13 Oct)

Coach's tip

The Day story gives you the side and the levels. It does not give you the timing. Let the trigger level and the scheduled events set the timing.

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Read the record chip before the flow chip. A flow that usually reverses is a fade, not a follow.
  • Compare the trigger distance in daily moves. Under 1 is in play today; over 3 is a later story.
  • Use the cross-asset reasons as live dashboards. If XLE moves with oil at corr +0.79, watch USO.
  • Light volume on a strong day asks for confirmation; heavy volume on a strong day earns attention.
  • Keep the AI narrative for orientation and the MEASURED rows for decisions.

Common mistakes

  • Treating "Money in" as a buy signal. It is a description of the last 5 sessions.
  • Reading a PUBLISHED item as fact. It is another LLM's claim, matched by keyword.
  • Reading "no chain" as "nothing unusual". Missing data tells you nothing.
  • Trading a stock-specific candidate because its sector flows the same way. A weak link means the sector does not carry it.
  • Ignoring "wrong if" levels that sit far away. A distant invalidation means a large loss before you are proved wrong.
