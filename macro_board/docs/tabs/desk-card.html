Desk card

For one ETF the pipeline proposes, does the board's evidence support the idea, is the entry still worth taking, and what is my decision?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

The Desk card is where reading turns into a decision. It takes one ETF and answers the six questions a trading desk asks before every trade: what changed, what should it affect, what is the market doing, what does the pipeline propose, is the entry still worth taking, and what would change my mind.

It then asks you to record your own decision: Act, Wait, Skip, or Insufficient information, with one sentence of reason. That record can be scored later, which is how you learn whether your judgement adds value.

Use it after the reading tabs. By the time you open the Desk card you should know the market's reaction (Daily review), where money is going (Day story), and the measured leans (Overview, ETF matrix). The card pulls those pieces together for one ticker.

The checks describe evidence. You decide.

No check on this card is a permission, a probability or a gate. A green check means "the evidence points the same way", not "go". The card never feeds the pipeline.

What it does

Which ETFs get a card

The four index ETFs (SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA) always get a card. So does every board ETF that the newest full pipeline run proposes as a CALL or a PUT. On 13 Oct that was 56 cards (run 20261013_221020). An ETF with missing prices gets no card.

Horizons

Every card is built three times, for the next 1, 5 and 20 sessions. A session is one trading day. The card opens on the pipeline's own horizon: the pipeline buckets 1_5d and 6_10d open at 5 sessions, 11_20d at 20 sessions, and anything else (for example OVER_20d) at the default of 20 sessions. That horizon is marked "·pipeline" on its button.

Where the facts come from

  • Pipeline proposal: direction, verdict, entry, target, invalidation, trigger state, contract, read-only from the newest full run.
  • Macro evidence: what the ETF did after past sessions whose macro conditions looked like today's ("analog" sessions), from the evidence engine.
  • Momentum evidence: what the ETF did after past sessions with the same streak or the same extension.
  • Option pricing: the ATM straddle from stored MarketData chains, compared with the move history delivered.
  • tastytrade metrics: IV rank and IV minus HV.
  • Condition changes, events and the news alert from the Overview and Macro intelligence tabs.

Terms you need

Trade terms

  • Target: where the pipeline expects the move to reach.
  • Invalidation: the price that proves the idea wrong.
  • Room: % left to the target against % left to the invalidation, from today's close.
  • Straddle: one ATM call plus one ATM put. Its price ÷ the stock price is the move the market prices.
  • IV rank: where today's implied volatility sits in its past-year range, 0 to 100.
  • IV − HV: implied volatility minus historical (realised) volatility, in points.

Evidence terms

  • Lean: Up or Down when past cases were clear enough; otherwise No clear lean.
  • Holdout: the last quarter of history, kept aside to check a lean was not fitted to the whole sample.
  • Independent windows: past cases counted without overlap.
  • 80% interval: the range that holds the true average 80% of the time, given the sample.
  • σ (sigma): a move measured in units of the ETF's own volatility.
  • t: how far an average sits from zero in standard errors.

How a lean is decided

  1. The board takes the forward return from the next session's open to the close h sessions later.
  2. It splits history: the first 75% is the train period, the last 25% is the holdout.
  3. On the train period it needs at least 8 independent windows, |t| of 1.5 or more, and an average move of at least 0.05% (1 session), 0.25% (5 sessions) or 0.75% (20 sessions). Then the lean is Up or Down; otherwise No clear lean.
  4. The holdout then agrees, disagrees, or is too thin (fewer than 3 independent windows).

All thresholds live in macro_board/config/macro_board_config_v1.json and are provisional.

Reading the screen

1. The candidate picker

A row of buttons: the index ETFs first, then the pipeline candidates. ▲ marks a CALL proposal, ▼ a PUT, · no direction. Click one to load its card.

2. The headline card

The ticker, its group, today's close with its date, and the pipeline chip ("CALL · ARMED"). On the right, the horizon buttons (1d, 5d, 20d). Under them, seven check pills in one row. Read the pills first: they are the summary. If a stale-target warning appears under them, read it before anything else.

3. The six questions

Six cards in reading order. Read 1 and 2 for the background, 3 for the tape, 4 for the proposal, 5 for the entry, and 6 for the exit plan.

4. Evidence for the next h sessions

A table with five rows: macro analog sessions, price only, and three momentum states. Compare each row's mean with the "Price only" row. A lean that only matches the ETF's normal drift adds nothing.

5. Your decision

Choose Act, Wait, Skip or Insufficient information. Write one sentence of reason using the template, what would change your mind, and your initials. Copy or download the record.

Scenarios

Scenario: everything agrees (illustrative)

What you see: For example, a CALL card at 5 sessions with Macro lean supports (✓ holdout), Momentum supports, Room left ok (ratio 1.8), Option price ok (0.92×), Data ok, Event risk ok, and Response Supporting.

What it means: The evidence points the same way as the proposal, the entry still leaves fair room, and options are not expensive.

What a trader does: Checks that the macro and momentum means clearly beat the price-only row, then considers Act at normal size with the pipeline invalidation as the exit. Records the decision.

Scenario: proposal against the tape and expensive options (XLK, 13 Oct)

What you see: XLK CALL · ARMED at 20 sessions. Room left against: 14.52% vs 35.46% (0.41). Option price against: 1.50× history, straddle spread 39.5% of mid. Response Mixed. The Day story shows XLK leaving a leader, with a reversed record.

What it means: The pipeline's invalidation (126.68) sits far below, so the risk to it is more than twice the reward to target. Options are dear and the quoted spread is very wide.

What a trader does: Wait. If the call idea is still wanted, a nearer exit level and a better quote are needed first. Records "Wait" with the room and spread as the reason.

Scenario: target already used up (LQD, 13 Oct)

What you see: LQD PUT. Room left against: 0.13% left to target vs 10.12% to invalidation (0.01). The stale-target warning: after similar runs LQD moved -0.49% over 20 sessions, more than the room left.

What it means: Price has almost reached the pipeline's target (102.42 against a close of 102.55). The measured drift says the move may go further, so the old target may be out of date.

What a trader does: Neither chases the old target nor dismisses the trend. Records "Wait" and asks for a re-evaluated target from the pipeline.

Scenario: nothing to do (SPY, 13 Oct)

What you see: SPY CALL · ARMED at 20 sessions. Macro lean, Momentum and Momentum (20d move) all no evidence. Room left caution (0.90). Option price ok (0.96×). Event risk caution: "Event verdict no trade; news alert amber". CPI prints at 08:30 ET.

What it means: No measured edge for the call, an even reward and risk, and a known event ahead.

What a trader does: Records "Wait": no evidence beyond drift, room under 1, CPI ahead. Revisits after the print.

Scenario: macro supports, but the holdout disagrees (XBI, 13 Oct)

What you see: XBI PUT · ARMED at 20 sessions. Macro lean supports, but the lean chip shows ✗ holdout and the interval runs -3.05% to +1.32%. Room left caution (0.95). Option price caution (1.16×, spread 28.7% of mid). Response Insufficient.

What it means: The green pill comes from the train period only. Out of sample, the lean did not hold, and the interval crosses zero.

What a trader does: Treats the macro check as no evidence. With caution on room and price, records "Skip" or "Wait".

Scenario: stale or missing inputs (illustrative)

What you see: For example, Data caution with "Prices fresh to 2026-10-13; macro packet stale; Money Index missing", or Option price unknown with "No option data for this ETF."

What it means: One or more inputs behind the card are old or absent. Missing is never neutral.

What a trader does: Records "Insufficient information", finds the stale input on Macro intelligence, and pulls a live chain before any option trade.

Applying it to a trade

Let's work through GLD (gold) at its pipeline horizon of 20 sessions, as a coach would with a new trader.

GLD · 20 sessionsCALL · ARMED
  1. Read the pills. Macro lean no evidence; Momentum (streak) supports; Momentum (20d move) supports; Room left ok; Option price caution; Data ok; Event risk caution. Coach: "Two greens, one ok room, two cautions. Let's see what the greens are made of."
  2. Test the greens against drift. The price-only row says GLD rose +1.37% over 20 sessions on average across all history. After 1-2 down closes it rose +1.45%; when not extended over 20 sessions, +1.19%. Coach: "Both momentum 'supports' are just gold's normal upward drift. The +1.19% is even below normal. They add almost nothing."
  3. Read the macro row. Sessions like today: +0.24%, interval -1.13% to +1.61%, 26 windows. Coach: "Today's macro backdrop has been worse than average for gold, though not clearly negative."
  4. Read the tape. Response Rejecting relief: the measured tailwind (curve move steepening, +2.06%, t 1.90) is active, yet GLD fell 3.17% over 20 sessions. It trails SPY by 5.89% over 20 sessions. Coach: "Good news for gold is not lifting it. That is a weakness signal."
  5. Read the proposal. Entry 381.67, target 509.70, invalidation 374.23, trigger early probe, contract 390 strike expiring 2026-12-18 (66 days) at 10.90. Stand-down reason: waiting for the morning re-quote. Coach: "The pipeline itself wants a fresh quote before it commits."
  6. Check the room honestly. 33.54% to target vs 1.95% to invalidation, ratio 17.21. The straddle prices a 4.64% move over 20 sessions. Coach: "The target is seven priced moves away, and the stop is well inside one. The great ratio comes from a distant target and a tight stop, not from a great entry. Normal noise can hit 374.23."
  7. Check the price. 1.27× history: options price more than gold has moved after similar sessions. IV rank 18, IV − HV +2.0. Coach: "Cheap against its own year, but rich against what this backdrop has delivered."
  8. Check the risk. Event risk caution: event verdict no trade, news alert amber, CPI at 08:30 ET.

Decision sentence: "Against a mixed tape with CPI ahead, gold is rejecting a measured tailwind and trailing SPY by 5.89% over 20 sessions; the pipeline proposes a GLD call, and at 381.67 the momentum support only matches normal drift, options price 1.27× history and the 374.23 invalidation sits inside one priced move, so I will wait because the evidence adds nothing beyond drift; I will change my mind if GLD's response turns supporting after the CPI print and the morning re-quote confirms the contract."

Coach's tip

A check pill tells you the direction of the evidence, not its size. Always compare a "supports" row with the "Price only" row before you count it.

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Read the pills, then the warning box, then the evidence table, then the proposal.
  • Compare the target and invalidation with the priced move in question 5.
  • Look at the holdout chip before you count a lean.
  • Switch horizons to match your holding period; the checks change with it.
  • Download every record, including the skips. Skips are data too.

Common mistakes

  • Reading green pills as "go". They describe evidence; they never decide.
  • Trusting a high room ratio without asking why the target is so far.
  • Ignoring a wide straddle spread: you pay it twice.
  • Treating "no evidence" as a mild positive. It is neutral.
  • Clearing a draft without downloading it: the draft lives only in your browser.
