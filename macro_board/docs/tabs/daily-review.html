Daily review

What happened in the last session, did the market believe the news, what is coming next, and where is the strength?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

The Daily review is the first tab the board opens. It turns yesterday's session into three plain questions: how did the market react to the news, what could force traders to rethink tomorrow, and where is the opportunity strongest.

It is your starting point every morning and every evening. Read it before the Day story, the Overview and the Next session plan. Those tabs go deeper; this one gives you the shape of the day.

It is written for a human trader. Nothing on it places a trade, ranks a stock for the pipeline or blocks anything. Macro and news on this board are display-only: they inform you, they never decide for you.

Coach's tipRead the tab in screen order. The reaction tells you what the market did; the decision points tell you what could change it; the opportunity section tells you where to look. Skipping straight to the opportunity section is the most common novice error.

What it does

The board builds this tab twice a session, from the evening build (after the close, the scored record) and the morning build (before the open). It reads only data it already holds.

Inputs

  • Daily bars (open, high, low, close) for each ETF (exchange-traded fund: a basket of shares that trades like one stock), from the pipeline's price store plus the board's own Polygon fill.
  • Issuer holdings files for SPY, QQQ, DIA and the 11 SPDR sector ETFs (what each fund owns and in what weight).
  • FRED (the St. Louis Fed's public economic database): release dates and the prints as known on the release day.
  • MarketData earnings dates for the largest holdings.
  • The event payload and the news enrichment: files written by an LLM (a large language model, here ChatGPT). They are shown as published and never measured.
  • GEX levels (dealer gamma estimates for SPY and QQQ) and the pipeline's own target and invalidation, used only as price levels.

Steps

  1. Daily move. For each instrument the board takes the standard deviation of daily log returns over the 20 sessions before the day. This is its normal daily swing, in percent. The day cannot inflate its own yardstick.
  2. Threshold. Half a daily move (0.5 × daily move, provisional, config review.threshold_daily_moves).
  3. Gap, path and close. Gap = open vs previous close. Open→close = close vs open. Close = close vs previous close.
  4. Reading. Absorbed if the close moved less than the threshold. Rejected if the open gapped beyond the threshold and the close finished beyond the threshold on the other side. Accepted otherwise.
  5. Record. For SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA it finds every past day with the same reading and measures what followed, from the next open to the close 1, 5 and 20 sessions later.
  6. Decision points. It lists scheduled events up to 20 sessions ahead and checks which ones, plus nearby price levels, fall on the next session.
  7. Opportunity. It compares QQQ with SPY, measures breadth, compares each sector with SPY, checks the sector's holdings, and finds the nearest price level above and below.
Display onlyEvery threshold on this tab is provisional (config file macro_board_config_v1.json). The labels describe behaviour. None of them is a probability or a permission to trade.

Reading the screen

The tab has three sections stacked top to bottom. Each table has a CSV button at its top right that downloads the table.

Section 1: The day's information and the market's reaction

A headline card opens the page, for example SPY absorbed · QQQ absorbed · IWM accepted (down) · DIA rejected (gap up) on 13 Oct. Read it first: it is the market's verdict on the session for the four index ETFs.

Below it, two cards sit side by side. How did markets react? lists cross-asset instruments, then the 11 sectors. Has this reading meant anything before? shows what followed past days with the same reading, and under it What disagreed? lists instruments that moved against SPY.

A wide card, What changed, and what was expected?, lists the news items and economic prints, with What remains unresolved at its foot.

Section 2: Tomorrow's decision points

First, one small card per index ETF answers "what could force participants to reassess this tomorrow?". Then the Scheduled, known in advance calendar, and the Unscheduled, unresolved developments table.

Section 3: Where the opportunity is strongest

A horizon selector (1d, 5d, 20d) sits at the top. It changes this section and also the window of the scheduled calendar above. Then Market: QQQ vs SPY with a breadth chip, Price location, and the Sectors vs SPY table.

Coach's tipWork top-down: market, then sectors, then constituents (the shares inside a fund), then price location. A sector looks strong only if the market around it and the shares inside it agree.

Scenarios

Scenario: a clean, agreeing day

What you see: for example, all four index ETFs read accepted ▲, SPY constituents "moved with SPY (72% of weight)", breadth chip broad, and the record table shows SPY's "accepted up" 5d average above the base with a lean UP and a holdout that agrees. Illustrative values.

What it means: buyers won across the board, the shares inside the index agreed, and this kind of day has been followed by more upside before.

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Size every move in daily moves. A 0.5% day in SPY is not the same as a 0.5% day in oil.
  • Look at the "win." count before the average. Fewer than 20 independent windows is thin.
  • Treat a lean with a disagreeing holdout as no lean.
  • On a flat SPY day, the disagreement list is the most useful part of the tab.
  • Set the horizon selector to your holding period before reading the sector table.
  • Note earnings marked AFTER_CLOSE: the reaction is in the next session.

Common mistakes

  • Reading "absorbed" as bearish or bullish. It means nothing was decided.
  • Buying calls on a sector that is "outperforming while falling".
  • Treating LLM rows (event payload, enrichment) as measured facts.
  • Reading a pipeline target in the Price location table as resistance.
  • Calling a FRED surprise a consensus surprise. The board measures it against the series' own history only.
  • Assuming "top contributors" in a falling sector are the names that caused the fall.
