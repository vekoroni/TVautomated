Method

Exactly how does the board turn data into the conditions, leans and labels on every other tab, and where are its limits?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

The other tabs show results. This tab shows the rules behind them: what each macro condition measures, when each data point becomes visible, how much evidence a lean needs, what each decision aid means, and what the board cannot know.

Read it once from top to bottom. After that, come back when a number on another tab surprises you, for example "oil fell 5% but the board says oil is STABLE" or "why is there no lean on TQQQ for 20 sessions?". The answer is almost always here.

For a trader, the Method tab is what separates a measured statement from a guess. If you know the rule, you know how much a reading can bear.

All thresholds are provisionalThe tab says so itself. Every number in the settings is a starting point, to be validated by the board's own scorecard. None of them is a law of markets.

What it does

The Method tab has no calculation of its own. It prints the board's live settings and definitions from the payload, so what you read always matches what the build used.

  • The Conditions table comes from the ten condition definitions in conditions.py.
  • The numbers in the notes come from the configuration blocks conditions, evidence, extension, response, holdings and the FRED publication rules, plus the outcome scorer's own trend, volatility and breadth settings (the "c12" settings shared with the pipeline's outcome scorer).
  • The latest FRED observation dates and the number of archived input files come from the current build.

Some key words used all through the tab:

  • Condition: one macro fact turned into a small set of states, such as 10Y yield RISING / STABLE / FALLING.
  • Analog sessions: past sessions whose conditions looked like today's.
  • Lean: a direction (UP or DOWN) that past analog sessions showed clearly enough to pass the rules. Otherwise "No clear lean".
  • Independent windows: the number of non-overlapping forward-return windows. Two sessions a day apart share 19 of 20 days of a 20-session window, so they are not two pieces of evidence.
  • Holdout: the newest 25% of history, kept aside so the board can test a lean on data it never saw.
  • t-statistic (t): the average divided by its uncertainty. Around 0 means noise; 1.5 or more is the board's bar for a lean.
  • Point in time: using only data that was public on that day, so history is not flattered by future information.

Reading the screen

1. Conditions (full-width card)

A table of the ten macro conditions: name, what is measured, its possible states and the data source. Below it, two notes: the exact windows and bands used, and the dates of the latest FRED observations on file.

2. Point in time

Five rules that stop the board from using data before it was public, and from treating missing data as neutral.

3. From evidence to lean

Five rules that decide whether a lean appears: how analog sessions are chosen, how windows are counted, what a lean needs, and the held-out check.

4. Decision aids (thesis review, 6 Oct)

Definitions of the labels you meet on the ETF matrix drawer, the Desk card and elsewhere: 80% interval, price-only lean, path-dependent, move made, desk card, response, priced vs history, pipeline, holdings, tastytrade and inputs.

5. Limits

Four honest limits. Read them before you trust any pattern.

Scenarios

Scenario: a lean that passes every rule

What you see: on 13 Oct, the measured Money Index band evidence for SPY 20d: lean UP, 33 independent windows, holdout agrees.

What it means: enough windows, a t above 1.5 on the older data, an average above 0.75%, and the newest quarter agreed. That is as clean as a lean gets on this board.

What a trader does: treats it as supporting context for an upside idea, never as a trigger. Still waits for price confirmation and checks Priced vs history before buying options.

Scenario: the label seems to contradict the number

What you see: 13 Oct: USO fell 5.57% in 20 sessions, yet Overview shows oil STABLE. And SPY 20d shows "Absorbing pressure" while the SPY 20d lean is "No clear lean".

What it means: oil's fall sits inside ±0.5 of its own normal 20-session variation. Response and lean answer different questions: response describes how SPY behaved under measured headwinds; lean asks whether history points a direction.

What a trader does: reads the definition before reacting. Does not call oil "stable" in the everyday sense; checks the Transmission map to see which ETFs oil has actually moved.

Scenario: data arrives late or not at all

What you see: 13 Oct: the dollar condition uses DEXUSEU from 9 Oct (released Tuesday after Columbus Day). For example, if FRED updates failed for a week, liquidity would show MISSING and analogs would match on nine conditions. (Second part illustrative.)

What it means: the board waits for publication and refuses to guess. MISSING is unknown, never neutral.

What a trader does: checks live FX or the missing series elsewhere before trading a view that depends on it. Gives less weight to leans on days with missing conditions.

Scenario: the analog set had to be loosened

What you see: 13 Oct: match level 60%. 100% and 90% found nothing, 70% gave only 11 windows, 60% gave 26.

What it means: today's combination of conditions is rare. The analogs share only 6 of 10 conditions with today.

What a trader does: treats every analog-based lean today as rough. Puts more weight on price, levels and the scenario records, which do not depend on the macro match.

Scenario: a path-dependent product

What you see: TQQQ 1d No clear lean, 20d Path-dependent.

What it means: TQQQ resets its 3× exposure daily, so multi-session history does not transfer. The board withholds the lean on purpose.

What a trader does: uses QQQ's evidence for the view and keeps any TQQQ position short-dated, sized for its daily swings.

Scenario: nothing to do

What you see: for example, the Method tab after a routine build: same settings, same limits, new FRED dates one day later. (Illustrative.)

What it means: the rules did not change.

What a trader does: nothing. The Method tab is reference. Come back when a reading elsewhere surprises you or when the configuration version changes.

Applying it to a trade

Coach: the ETF matrix shows SPY 20d "No clear lean". A friend says "macro is bullish, history says SPY rises". Let us use the Method rules to see exactly why the board stays quiet.

  1. Which conditions? Today: SPY trend ABOVE BOTH, realised vol LOW, breadth LOW, liquidity FLAT, curve level FLAT, curve move STEEPENING, 10Y RISING, credit STABLE, dollar STRENGTHENING, oil STABLE. None MISSING.
  2. How close are the analogs? The board loosened to 60%: 111 past sessions match at least 6 of the 10, giving 26 independent 20-session windows.
  3. What did they do? SPY analog mean +0.42% over 20 sessions, up 61% of the time. All days: +0.92%. So these analogs did slightly worse than an average month.
  4. Does it pass the lean rules? On the train period (before 2025-06-10): 21 windows, mean +0.40%, t 0.46. It needs |t| ≥ 1.5 and |mean| ≥ 0.75%. It fails both. Result: No clear lean.
  5. How uncertain? 80% interval −0.53% … +1.37%. It crosses zero.
  6. What does price alone say? Price-only lean: also No clear lean.
  7. Stage and price. Move made over 20 sessions: 1.0σ, not extended. Priced vs history 20d: 0.96 (a 31-day straddle prices 2.84% for 20 sessions vs 2.95% delivered by analogs). Fairly priced.
SPY, 20 sessions, 13 Octmatch 60% · 26 windows · train t 0.46 · No clear lean

Macro history gives no direction for SPY over the next month. The run is not extended, and options are priced close to what similar sessions delivered.

Decision sentence: "The board's macro evidence is neutral for SPY at 20 sessions, so any SPY trade rests on price structure and my own trigger; I do not quote 'macro is bullish' as a reason." This is a reading of the rules, not a recommendation.

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Learn the four lean gates: 8 windows, |t| 1.5, a minimum average, and the holdout.
  • When a label surprises you, find its row here before you argue with it.
  • Watch the analog match level on Overview: 60% means rough analogs.
  • "Stable" and "No clear lean" are real answers. They tell you macro is not the story today.
  • Use Priced vs history every time you plan to buy options.

Common mistakes

  • Reading RISING or FALLING as a level. They describe a fast 20-session change.
  • Counting analog sessions instead of independent windows.
  • Treating MISSING as neutral.
  • Holding a leveraged or inverse fund on a 20-session macro view.
  • Forgetting that thresholds are provisional and history covers one rate cycle.
  • Assuming the "Latest FRED observations" dates are the ones in use.
