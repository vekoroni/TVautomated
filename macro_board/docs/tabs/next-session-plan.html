Next session plan

Putting every piece of the board together: what is the call for tomorrow, which levels decide it, what do I check and when, and does each input agree?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

The Next session plan is the last tab and the one you take into the next session. It arranges the board's pieces in the order an experienced trader would use them: the call, the scenarios, a time-ordered checklist, where the opportunity is, what would change the view, each ETF on its own, the pipeline's candidates, and finally the jigsaw of every input.

Read it last, after the Daily review and the Overview, in the evening or before the open. Print the checklist, or keep it beside your trading screen.

Every sentence on it is assembled from measured fields using fixed wording from config. No LLM writes it. It never scores, ranks for the pipeline or gates anything. "Action (you decide)" columns describe evidence; the decision is yours.

Coach's tipA plan is a set of if-then statements, not a prediction. "If SPY loses 768.60, the down case is in play" is a plan. "SPY will fall" is a guess.

What it does

Inputs

The plan reads only the board's assembled data: the Daily review's reactions and levels, the Overview's leans and option prices, the decision points calendar, the overnight capture, the Scenario watch's live triggers, the local Money Index, the advisory files, and the pipeline's newest run (read-only). Wording lives in config session_plan; every threshold there is provisional.

Steps

  1. The tape. The "tape" is the market's own action. The board reads how SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA closed: accepted up and rejected gap down count as up; accepted down and rejected gap up count as down; absorbed counts as no direction. Three of four in one direction sets UP or DOWN, otherwise MIXED. Fewer than three readable gives UNKNOWN.
  2. The call. The tape is joined with breadth (broad, narrow, unknown) into a key such as UP_BROAD, with a matching approach sentence from config.
  3. Scenarios. For each index ETF it takes the current price and the board's levels (252-day high and low, 50- and 200-day averages, GEX walls and flip, pipeline target and invalidation). The nearest level above is the up trigger; the nearest below is the down trigger. The next level beyond is the target, unless it is more than 5 daily moves away; then the target is one implied move beyond the trigger and the far level is shown as "next mapped level".
  4. History. It adds what followed the same reaction and the same macro conditions over 1 and 5 sessions. A scenario is marked "history leans here" only where the reaction history measured a lean.
  5. Expression. It compares the 5-session straddle's price with the move history delivered: cheap below 0.9×, rich above 1.1×. The runway (time to buy) is twice the horizon: 10 sessions.
  6. Checklist, opportunity, change view. It sorts tomorrow's items by time, lists sector strength and weakness, and lists the closes that would cancel each case.
  7. Per ETF and candidates. For every board ETF and for the pipeline's top 200 candidates it runs a set of checks (tape, Money Index band, macro analogs, relative strength, room) and labels the result aligned, mixed or against.
  8. The jigsaw. It lists 20 inputs, says which way each points, and whether it supports or opposes the tape.

Reading the screen

Title bar

"Next session plan", the session pair (for example "2026-10-13 close → 2026-10-14") and two buttons on the right: Copy sheet for ChatGPT and sheet .md.

The call

One card with a coloured key chip, three or four measured sentences, the Approach, an orange conflict note when narratives disagree with measurement, and the payload's unresolved verdict.

Scenarios

One card each for SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA with Up, Base and Down rows, the Expression line and a short history list.

Checklist

A time-ordered table for the next session: before the open, at the open, during the session, after the close, and things to watch.

Where the opportunity is / What would change the view

Two cards side by side: leadership, sector strength and weakness, pipeline ETFs against the tape; and the list of view-changing closes.

ETF by ETF, Pipeline candidates, The jigsaw

Three wide tables at the bottom. Hover a "Reading" chip to see the checks behind it.

Coach's tipRead the call and your instrument's scenario card, then the checklist. Only then open the ETF by ETF and candidates tables. They are long (115 and 200 rows on 13 Oct); go straight to the rows you trade.

Scenarios

Scenario: everything lines up

What you see: for example, the call reads Up, broad, the SPY Up row shows "history leans here", the expression chip is "cheap vs history", and the jigsaw shows Measured 7 support, 1 oppose, 3 neutral. Illustrative values.

What it means: buyers won broadly, history after this reaction leaned up, options are reasonably priced, and most measured inputs agree.

What a trader does: plans long calls or shares on a hold of the first level below, with an expiry beyond the 10-session runway, and exits on a close below the "idea is wrong below" level.

Scenario: the pipeline wants calls, the plan says wait (13 Oct 2026)

What you see: SPY "CALL · ARMED" in the ETF by ETF table with the reading against. Hovering shows the room check against: "GEX flip 779.82 is 0.04 daily moves above". Action: "wait for SPY to clear GEX flip 779.82, or stand aside."

What it means: the pipeline's idea has a level directly overhead and no supporting tape.

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Write your three if-then lines from the scenario card before the open.
  • Check the trigger's distance in daily moves. Over 3 is unlikely in one session.
  • Use the expression chip to choose options or shares, never the direction.
  • Hover the Reading chip; one "against" check (often room) can explain the whole reading.
  • For candidates, read the Link column before the sector checks.
  • Keep the CSV of each plan and compare it with what happened.

Common mistakes

  • Treating "history leans here" as a prediction. Check the case count (DIA's rested on 20 windows).
  • Treating a pipeline target in a scenario as a market level.
  • Reading jigsaw counts as odds. They are counts.
  • Letting LLM pieces outvote measured ones.
  • Applying a sector read to a stock-specific candidate.
  • Buying options that expire inside the runway.
