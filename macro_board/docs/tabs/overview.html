Overview

What happened overnight, what does history say about SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA under today's macro conditions, and which conditions are we in?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

The Overview is the board's dashboard for the four index ETFs (SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA) and the macro backdrop. It answers three questions: what moved since the US close, what each index has done after past sessions that looked like today, and which of ten macro conditions are in force.

Read it after the Daily review and the Day story, and before you look at single ETFs on the ETF matrix. In the morning, the Overnight block at the top is the first thing to check.

Every lean on this tab is a description of recorded history. It is not a forecast and it is not permission to trade. The LLM packet at the bottom is advisory and shown as published.

Coach's tipThe header line and the Inputs strip appear on every tab. Learn them here once: they tell you whether the numbers below can be trusted today.

What it does

Inputs

  • Daily bars for every ETF from the pipeline's price store, with gaps filled by the board from Polygon grouped daily bars. A latest price layer takes one Polygon snapshot (about 15 minutes delayed) on refresh builds.
  • FRED series for liquidity, the yield curve, the 10-year yield and the dollar, each aligned to the day it was published.
  • The overnight capture, a JSON file written by the macro inputs runner: futures, pre-market, VIX, FX, Asia, Europe and the 08:30 ET prints.
  • Option chains (MarketData quotes stored in the phantom database), tastytrade metrics (read-only), issuer holdings, the GEX file and the pipeline's proposals from its newest run.
  • The LLM macro packet (advisory).

Steps

  1. Conditions. For every past session the board sets each of ten conditions to a state (for example the 10-year yield RISING, STABLE or FALLING). "Rising" means the 20-session change was more than 0.5 standard deviations above zero, measured against that series' own last year (a z-score). Stale inputs become MISSING, never their last value.
  2. Analogs. It finds past sessions whose conditions match today's. It starts at a 100% match and loosens to 90%, 80%, 70% and 60% until it has at least 12 independent windows (non-overlapping 20-session stretches).
  3. Evidence. For each ETF and horizon (1, 5, 20 sessions) it measures the return from the next session's open to the close h sessions later, after every analog session, and compares it with all sessions.
  4. Lean and holdout. It decides a lean on the first three quarters of history and checks it on the last quarter (the holdout).
  5. Decision aids. It measures how far each ETF has already moved, and whether price agrees with the macro pressure measured for it.
  6. Option price. It reads the at-the-money straddle (a call plus a put at the same strike) to see how big a move the option market is charging for.

The tab updates on every build. The morning build adds the overnight capture at the top; the evening build is the scored record.

Reading the screen

Header line, authority banner and Inputs strip

At the very top: the session, the build kind and the build time. Under it, a grey banner states what the board is and is not. Then the Inputs strip: one chip per source with ✓ (fresh), ! (stale) or × (missing). Scan the strip for any ! or × before reading anything else.

Overnight

Cards for US index futures, US pre-market ETFs, VIX overnight, FX and the dollar, rates and commodity futures, Asia and Europe, plus "US prints today" from FRED. On a morning build, this is your first read.

Index focus

Four cards: SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA. Each shows price, recent returns, the pipeline's proposal, measured leans per horizon, the option market's price for movement, tastytrade volatility, holdings and dealer gamma. Hover any chip for its evidence.

Macro conditions now

Ten tiles, one per condition, each with its current state, value, a sparkline, a colour strip of past states and the date of the data used.

What changed, Analog depth, Recent analog episodes, Advisory packet headline

Four cards at the bottom: recent state changes, how closely today is matched to history, what the indexes did after the latest similar episodes, and the LLM packet's headline.

Hidden settingThe Overview has no horizon selector of its own. The Response chip, the tastytrade "IV-implied |move|" and the holdings line use the horizon you last picked on another tab (Daily review, ETF matrix, Transmission map or Sector rotation). It is 20d by default.

Scenarios

Scenario: macro and price agree

What you see: for example, SPY shows ▲ Up ✓ holdout at 5d and 20d, analog interval +0.3% to +1.2% at 5d (clear of zero), Response "Supporting", Not extended, option ratio 0.85×, SPY trend ABOVE_BOTH and breadth HIGH. Illustrative values.

What it means: today-like sessions have led higher, the lean survived the holdout, price is behaving as the macro suggests, and options are not expensive.

What a trader does: treats this as a supportive backdrop for longs, then goes to the Next session plan for levels and the Desk card for the exact candidate. Still sizes by the daily move.

Scenario: the pipeline and the evidence disagree (13 Oct 2026)

What you see: SPY "CALL · ARMED" from the pipeline, but no clear lean on any horizon, Response 20d "Absorbing pressure" (headwinds Realised volatility and Curve level), and the packet's regime "TRANSITIONAL BEARISH" next to a "SELECTIVE RISK ON" switch.

What it means: the pipeline wants calls, the measured macro history gives no direction, and buyers have been overriding measured headwinds.

What a trader does: waits. Uses the Desk card to see the pipeline's trigger, and lets price confirm a level first. Size smaller if acting at all.

Scenario: stale or missing inputs

What you see: on 13 Oct, QQQ holdings stale (7 sessions old, 4.2% weight unpriced), VIX overnight 103m with "!", and Liquidity observed 4 sessions ago. For example, on another day the Overnight block could read "× MISSING", or a condition tile "× MISSING". Mixed real and illustrative.

What it means: some numbers describe an older market. A missing condition means analogs match on fewer conditions.

What a trader does: discounts the stale pieces, checks live futures and VIX before the open, and does not lean on QQQ's holdings checks until the file refreshes.

Scenario: nothing to do (13 Oct 2026)

What you see: all four ETFs show "No clear lean" on 1d, 5d and 20d; analog match fell to 60%; recent episodes disagree in sign (SPY +2.53%, −1.81%, −2.35%); futures flat (median −0.02%).

What it means: the macro backdrop is unusual and history is split. The Overview offers no direction.

What a trader does: takes no macro-driven position. Moves on to levels (Next session plan) and to the measured movers (Day story). A quiet Overview is common.

Scenario: options look expensive, and the spread is wide (13 Oct 2026, DIA)

What you see: DIA 1d row: 3d · 516.0, priced move 0.67% vs analog |move| 0.59%, ratio 1.14×, spread 5.9% of mid.

What it means: the short-dated straddle charges more than similar days delivered, and the bid-ask spread eats almost 6% of the premium.

What a trader does: avoids short-dated DIA options for a 1-day idea. Prefers shares or a longer expiry with a tighter spread (DIA 20d spread 3.7%).

Scenario: the move is already extended

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Read the Inputs strip first, every time.
  • Trust a lean only with ✓ holdout and an interval clear of zero.
  • Compare Analog with Base. The gap is what today's conditions add.
  • Check the match level. At 60%, analogs may share as few as 6 of 10 conditions.
  • Use the option Ratio to choose between options and shares, not to choose a direction.
  • Remember the Overview uses the horizon picked on another tab.

Common mistakes

  • Reading "No clear lean" as bearish. It means no direction.
  • Treating the pipeline chip as a signal to act.
  • Treating GEX walls and flip as measured dealer positions. They are model estimates.
  • Reading an evening capture as the overnight move.
  • Reading the packet's conviction or probability as a real probability.
  • Ignoring the spread column when the ratio looks cheap.
