Scenario watch

Which written-down trade ideas are live right now, what exactly would trigger them, and how did a long option on that idea pay after every past trigger?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

Traders carry rules of thumb in their heads: "if QQQ cannot hold a yearly high for 3 sessions, buy puts". Scenario watch writes each rule down, dates it, and tests it on every ETF's full history. It then tells you which rules are live today and how close each one is to triggering.

The test is the one that matters for long options: did buying an option on the idea pay after costs, compared with buying the same option on any day? A rule can be right about direction and still lose money on options, because options lose value with time and cost a spread to trade.

Use it after the Desk card and the Day story, when you want to know whether a setup you see has a record. Live index-ETF scenarios also appear in the Next session plan checklist.

Display only

No scenario scores, ranks or gates anything. A verdict describes history; it is not a forecast or a permission. Registering a scenario before looking at its result, and keeping it even if it fails, is what keeps the record honest.

What it does

The library

Each scenario lives in scenario_watch.library in the board config, with an id, a name, a side (PUT or CALL), a registration date, a source and a hypothesis. On 13 Oct there were 10 scenarios, all registered on 2026-10-12. Each is tested on every ETF with at least 500 sessions of history: 77 ETFs.

Trigger parts

Every trigger is built from five fixed parts. A daily move here is the standard deviation of the 20 daily log returns before the day, in %. It leaves the day itself out, so a big day cannot inflate its own yardstick.

  • failed_extreme: a new closing high (or low) over the lookback, with no higher (lower) close in the next N sessions. An optional depth asks the last close to sit at least some daily moves away.
  • held_extreme: a new closing high (low) that is still at or above (below) that level N sessions later.
  • near_extreme: the close within N daily moves of the lookback high (low).
  • streak: exactly N up (or down) closes in a row.
  • big_day: a close at least N daily moves up (or down).

Optional conditions require one or more of the 10 macro condition states on the trigger day, for example breadth LOW or credit WIDENING.

How each past trigger is scored

  1. Entry and exit. Entry at the next session's open; exit at the close h sessions after the trigger, for h = 1, 3, 5 and 10.
  2. Move for the hypothesis. The return in the scenario's direction: positive means the idea was right. For a PUT, a 1% fall counts as +1%.
  3. First passage. Did price reach one expected move (daily move × √h) in favour before one expected move against, on closes?
  4. Excursions. The best and worst close along the way, in the scenario's direction.
  5. Option proxy. A long at-the-money option, priced with the Black-Scholes formula (a standard option pricing model). Expiry is 2× the horizon. Implied volatility is the 20-session realised volatility × 1.15. It is bought at the ask and sold at the bid with a 3% round-trip spread on premium. The result is the % gain or loss on the premium. No historical quotes are used.
  6. Every-session baseline. The same option bought on every session in history. Most of the time this loses, because time decay and the spread cost money. A scenario earns its keep only if it beats this.

Verdicts

  • Too thin: fewer than 8 independent windows (past triggers counted without overlapping outcomes).
  • Edge: the option proxy beat buying every session, with t of 1.5 or more. t measures how far the average sits from zero in standard errors.
  • Opposite side paid: the move went against the hypothesis, with t of -1.5 or less.
  • No edge: neither.

A holdout check then looks at the last quarter of triggers on their own: it agrees, disagrees, or is too thin (fewer than 3 independent windows).

The false-discovery check

Testing 10 scenarios × 77 ETFs × 4 horizons means 3,080 tries. With that many tries, some results look strong by luck. The board runs the Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery check at 10%: it ranks all results by how surprising they are and keeps only those strong enough to stand out after counting every try. Results that pass are marked "beats the tries"; the rest are "within chance".

Live status and the live record

For every scenario and ETF the board also checks today: Triggered on the last close, Watching (close to triggering, with level and deadline), Partial (price met, macro condition not), or Not active. Pre-open and evening builds write each new trigger once to output/scenario_ledger.jsonl. Later builds score it at each horizon as it completes. That is the out-of-sample record: results the scenario could not have been fitted to.

All thresholds are in scenario_watch and evidence in the config. The option proxy is provisional until stored chains can replace it.

Reading the screen

1. Header and the option-proxy box

The box restates what a scenario is and how the option proxy is built (runway 2×, IV = realised vol × 1.15, 3% spread). Read it once.

2. The false-discovery banner

On 13 Oct: 3,080 results tested; 72 read Edge and 232 Opposite side paid at the per-result bar; after the check, 1 edge and 6 opposites survive. Coach: "Out of 304 strong-looking results, 7 stand out after counting the tries. That ratio is your humility check."

3. Controls

Horizon buttons (1d, 3d, 5d, 10d; 5d by default) change every record on the page. The "index ETFs only" box filters the live table to SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA.

4. Live now

The heart of the tab. Triggered rows first, then Watching, then Partial; index ETFs first within each. On 13 Oct: 59 live rows (9 triggered, 42 watching, 8 partial). Click a row to open its detail.

5. Detail panel

The scenario's state, a table for every horizon, the last 8 triggers and the exact trigger definition.

6. Scenario library

One card per scenario: hypothesis, registration, verdict counts at the chosen horizon, and a cell per ETF coloured by verdict. Click any cell for its detail.

7. Live record

Every recorded live trigger, newest first, with its result at each horizon or "pending".

8. Adding your own scenario

Instructions for registering a new scenario in the config, with the trigger parts listed.

Scenarios

Scenario: a clean, surviving edge (VXX big up day reverses, 1d)

What you see: Edge · holdout ✓ · beats the tries. 62 triggers, 62 independent. Option proxy +47.0% per trade vs -11.0% buying every session (edge +58.0%, t 3.91). Wins 60%, average win +118%, average loss -59%. On 13 Oct its state is Not active.

What it means: After VXX (a volatility product) closed up at least 2 daily moves, a 1-session put on the proxy paid well on average, held out of sample, and survives counting all 3,080 tries. It is the only Edge that survives on the board.

What a trader does: Nothing today, because it is not live. Adds a note: if VXX closes up 2 or more daily moves, check a live chain the next morning, since the proxy assumes a 3% spread and real VXX option costs may differ.

Scenario: several scenarios disagree on one ETF (QUAL, 13 Oct)

What you see: QUAL has four live rows. "At the high on narrow breadth" PUT: Triggered, Too thin (6 / 4). "Failed to hold a yearly high" PUT: Watching (227.72, 2 closes left, deadline 2026-10-15), Opposite side paid · holdout ✓ · within chance at 5d. "Held a yearly high for 3 sessions" CALL: Watching, No edge, but its Edge column reads -26.6% (t -2.90). "At the high while credit weakens" PUT: Partial.

What it means: Put ideas at QUAL's highs have tended to be wrong on direction, yet the call counterpart has lost more than random timing after costs. Price drifted up, but not enough to pay for a 10-session option.

What a trader does: Skips both option ideas. If anything, the record argues against buying puts at QUAL's highs. A trader wanting the upside would consider shares, which carry no time decay, rather than calls.

Scenario: triggered but too thin (SPY narrow high, 13 Oct)

What you see: SPY "At the high on narrow breadth" PUT: Triggered on the 2026-10-13 close. At 5d: Too thin, 6 triggers, 4 independent, option proxy -14.0% vs -16.1% every session. At 1d: No edge, 9 / 9, -27.4% vs -14.2%.

What it means: The setup is rare. Where there is enough data (1 day), the put did worse than buying puts at random.

What a trader does: Does not act on the scenario. Uses the Day story and Next session plan levels for SPY instead.

Scenario: waiting on a macro condition (SPY credit divergence, 13 Oct)

What you see: Partial: "Price condition met on the 2026-10-13 close; waiting for credit WIDENING (now STABLE)." At 5d: No edge, 94 triggers, 31 independent, -25.8% vs -16.1%.

What it means: SPY is near its yearly high, but credit is not weakening. Even when both held in the past, puts did worse than random.

What a trader does: Notes the credit tile on the Overview. A flip to WIDENING would trigger the scenario, but its record gives no reason to act on it alone.

Scenario: nothing to do on the index ETFs

What you see: With "index ETFs only" ticked, the live table shows just SPY's two rows. QQQ, IWM and DIA have nothing live.

What it means: No registered rule is close to firing on three of the four index ETFs, and SPY's two have no measured edge.

What a trader does: Moves on. A quiet Scenario watch is a normal day.

Scenario: the live record disagrees with history (illustrative and real)

What you see: The live record shows SOXX "Five down closes in a row" PUT triggered 2026-10-12: next day -2.5% for the hypothesis, option -82%. For example, imagine a scenario marked Edge in history whose first ten live triggers mostly lost.

What it means: One live result proves nothing. A run of live results that disagree with history suggests the historical edge was luck or has faded.

What a trader does: Keeps the scenario, keeps watching the live record, and sizes down any trade it suggests until live results match history.

Applying it to a trade

Let's work through the most actionable-looking live row on 13 Oct, QUAL "Failed to hold a yearly high", as a coach would.

QUAL · Failed to hold a yearly highPUT · Watching
  1. Read the state. "New 252-session closing high 227.72 on 2026-10-12. Triggers if the next 2 close(s) stay below 227.72 (deadline 2026-10-15)." QUAL closed at 227.09 on 13 Oct. Coach: "One close down, two to go. If QUAL closes above 227.72 before 15 Oct, the watch ends."
  2. Read the definition. failed_extreme, high, lookback 252, hold 3 sessions, no depth requirement. Coach: "Any close below the high counts, even by a cent."
  3. Read the verdict at 5d. Opposite side paid · holdout ✓ · within chance. Coach: "History says puts here were wrong on direction, and the newest quarter agrees. But with 3,080 tries, this could be luck."
  4. Read the numbers. 47 triggers, 43 independent. Option proxy -28.9% per trade vs -17.6% buying every session: edge -11.3%. Wins 28%, average win +52%, average loss -60%. Move for the hypothesis -0.41% (right 38%). Coach: "Puts lost more than random puts. Wins were rarer and smaller than losses."
  5. Check across horizons. 1d and 3d: No edge. 10d: Opposite side paid again, move -0.81%. Coach: "The longer you hold, the more QUAL drifted up against the put."
  6. Check the paths. In favour first 13%, against first 19%; best +0.7%, worst -0.9%. Coach: "Even good trades rarely ran far."
  7. Check macro like today. 4 triggers (4 independent), +21.0%. Coach: "Tempting, but four cases is a story, not evidence."
  8. Check the counterpart. "Held a yearly high for 3 sessions" CALL on QUAL: No edge, -29.2% vs -2.6% every session. Coach: "So the 'opposite side' does not mean calls paid. Time decay and the spread ate them too."

Decision sentence: "QUAL is watching a failed yearly high at 227.72 with a 15 Oct deadline; past triggers saw puts lose 28.9% per trade against 17.6% for random puts, the hypothesis was wrong 62% of the time and the result does not beat the tries, so I will skip the put; I will change my mind only if the live record builds a run of winning triggers."

Coach's tip

For long options, the question is never "was the direction right?" alone. It is "did the option pay more than buying the same option on any day?" That is what the Edge column answers.

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Read the false-discovery banner every day. It reminds you how many results are luck.
  • Prefer results that hold across horizons and have holdout ✓.
  • Always compare Option proxy with Every session; both are usually negative.
  • Look at the Edge column even when the chip says No edge.
  • Use Watching rows to set alerts: the level and the deadline are exact.

Common mistakes

  • Acting on "Edge" that is dashed or "within chance".
  • Reading "Opposite side paid" as "buy the other side". It describes the underlying move, not an option on the other side.
  • Trusting "Macro like today" with a handful of cases.
  • Forgetting the proxy has no real quotes: a wide real spread can erase a small edge.
  • Editing or deleting a scenario because it failed. That breaks the honest record.
