Macro intelligence

How much risk-off pressure is the market under, measured from our own data, and what are the outside macro reports saying, how old is each one, and do they agree?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

Every trader hears a constant stream of macro talk: rates, oil, the VIX, credit, the Fed, geopolitics. This tab puts that stream in one place, labels where each piece came from, and shows how fresh it is.

It has two parts. The top part is the US Money Index, measured here: the board scores seven risk-off signals from data AVSHUNTER holds and checks what happened next in the past when the score sat in today's band. The lower part, Macro intelligence, shows the outside reports exactly as they were published: an LLM macro packet, the published Money Index, an event report, a news overlay, the VIX complex, dealer gamma estimates, rates and credit, liquidity, Colab models, FRED economic prints and series flags.

Read it after the Daily review, Overview and Scenario watch, when you already know what price did. Use it to answer one question: is the macro backdrop a headwind, a tailwind or noise for the trade you are considering? Then check the Scorecards tab before giving any outside source weight.

Display onlyNothing on this tab feeds a gate, score or rank, on the board or in the pipeline. It informs a human. The outside reports are advisory: most were written by a language model (an LLM, a text-generating AI) or by a script, and many of their scores carry an uncalibrated label, meaning nobody has checked them against outcomes, so they are not probabilities.

What it does

Part 1: the measured Money Index

The published US Money Index is a 0 to 100 "risk-off transmission" score made by ChatGPT from nine weighted components. Risk-off means money leaving risky assets (stocks, high-yield bonds) for safety. Transmission means stress spreading from one market to others. Seven of the nine components can be measured from data the board already holds, so the board scores them itself:

  1. Collect seven raw series for every session: oil (USO 20-session change), the 10-year Treasury yield change over 20 sessions in basis points (a basis point, bp, is 0.01 percentage points), credit appetite (HYG vs LQD over 20 sessions), the VIX close from FRED, breadth (share of stored tickers above their 50-session average), small caps (IWM vs SPY over 20 sessions) and AI/semis leadership (SMH vs SPY over 5 sessions).
  2. Rank each one against its own past year. The board takes today's value and finds its percentile (where it sits from 0 to 100% among the last 252 sessions, needing at least 126). It flips the ranking for components where a low value is the risk-off side (credit, breadth, small caps, AI/semis), so a higher percentile always means more risk-off.
  3. Turn the percentile into points. At or below the 50th percentile (the median) a component scores 0. At the 95th percentile or above it scores its full weight. In between the points rise in a straight line. Example: rates at the 88th percentile with weight 10 score (0.88 − 0.50) ÷ (0.95 − 0.50) × 10 = 8.3 points.
  4. Add up and rescale. The seven weights total 76 of the published 100. The board divides the points by 76 and multiplies by 100 to get a 0 to 100 score. If any component is missing, there is no total for that session: missing is never counted as zero.
  5. Band it. Below 35 is calm, 35 to under 60 is transition, 60 and above is risk-off. These bands are provisional settings in the configuration.
  6. Measure what followed. For SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA the board looks at every past session in today's band and measures the return from the next session's open to the close 1, 5 and 20 sessions later. It uses the board's evidence rules (independent windows, a lean fitted on older data, a held-out check), and it computes an information coefficient (IC): the rank correlation between the score and the next 5-session return.
  7. Compare with the published score component by component. Geopolitics and Hormuz (12 points each) cannot be measured from price data, so they stay "news only". The comparable total adds the board's measured points to the published geopolitics and Hormuz points, over all 100 weights.

Part 2: the outside reports

Each build reads the newest copy of each outside file from the dropbox folders. For every file the board records its as-of time, its age in hours and a status: FRESH (within its age limit), STALE (older), MISSING (no file) or UNREADABLE (the file would not parse). The age limit is 30 hours for most files, 48 hours for the published Money Index and the event payload, and 8 days for the weekly Fung-Hsieh filter. For CSV files the age comes from the file's save time, and the as-of shown is the newest date inside the file.

The board then picks the fields a human needs from each file and shows them as published. It does not re-calculate them. Where a source is MISSING or UNREADABLE, its card shows dashes.

The page updates on every board build. Pre-open and evening builds pick up the newest outside files. The 13 Oct example on this page is a morning build showing the 13 Oct close.

Reading the screen

1. US Money Index, measured here (heading row)

Start here. The heading says what is measured: 7 of the 9 published components, each against its own past year, beside the published (LLM) packet. Three buttons sit on the right: CSV, Copy sheet for ChatGPT and sheet .md.

2. Risk-off transmission card (left)

Look at the band chip first, then the big number, then the line under it that compares the published score. The small line chart under it shows how the measured score has moved over the last 260 sessions. Read the grey note to see how often each band has occurred.

3. What followed today's band (right)

This answers "when our measured index was in this band before, what did the index ETFs do next?" Each cell shows the band's average return, then "vs" the average of all days. Hover a cell for the sample size. The last column, IC 5d, tells you whether the score has ever ranked future returns.

4. The component table

One row per component, nine in total. Read across: today's raw value, its percentile, the points the board gives it, and the points the published packet gives it. Big gaps between measured and published points show where the LLM sees more (or less) stress than the data.

5. Macro intelligence (outside reports)

A grid of cards, each ending in a source line with a status chip, the file path, its as-of time and its age. Read the source line before reading the card. Order on screen: LLM macro packet, published US Money Index, Event risk, News overlay, Volatility complex, Dealer gamma, Rates and credit, Liquidity monitor, Colab models. Below the grid sit two wide tables: Economic prints (FRED) and Series threshold flags.

Dealer gamma

GEX (gamma exposure) estimates how option dealers must hedge as price moves. When dealers are long gamma (positive), they sell rallies and buy dips, which damps moves. When short gamma (negative), they hedge in the same direction as the move, which can amplify it.

Scenarios

Scenario: measured and published agree on calm

What you see: for example, measured score 24 calm, published 30 (stress unconfirmed), comparable total about 28, VIX in contango, GEX positive, all sources FRESH. (Illustrative values.)

What it means: data and outside opinion agree that macro is not adding pressure.

What a trader does: lets the price setup lead. Macro gives no reason to cut size. Moves on to the Next session plan and the triggers there.

Scenario: the data and the LLM disagree (13 Oct)

What you see: measured 55 transition, published 84, comparable total 66. Credit measured 6.8/14 vs published 14/14. Oil measured 0/12 vs published 6/12 and Hormuz 12/12. The packet's regime says bearish while its direction bias says mildly bullish.

What it means: real stress sits in breadth, small caps and semis. The LLM adds stress in credit, oil and news that prices do not yet show. The packet contradicts itself.

What a trader does: trusts the measured components for what is happening, and treats the extra published points as risks to watch. Checks "What followed": transition sessions were followed by above-average 20-session returns for SPY. So no fresh short thesis from macro alone. Smaller size on broad index longs until breadth repairs; for semis, waits for price to confirm.

Scenario: fresh file, old content

What you see: on 13 Oct the bond state source line says FRESH, but its curve block is dated 2026-10-09 and its own warning says "Yield curve data STALE". The GEX data is dated 2026-10-12. The published Money Index is 31 h old and carries an S&P 500 figure dated 2026-09-25.

What it means: FRESH only means the file was saved recently. Values inside can be days or weeks old.

What a trader does: reads the dates inside every card before quoting a number. Uses the FRED prints table and the measured 10Y component for rates. Treats GEX levels as yesterday's map. Does not repeat an undated or weeks-old fact as current.

Scenario: a source is missing

What you see: for example, × MISSING on the event payload and dashes on the Event risk card, or "missing: volatility" in red on the measured index with a dash for the score. (Illustrative.)

What it means: the board does not know. A missing component never becomes zero, and a missing report is not "no events".

What a trader does: treats macro pressure as unknown, not calm. Checks the scheduled events on the Daily review decision calendar directly. Sizes as if risk were higher until the gap is filled.

Scenario: dealer gamma negative, spot below the flip

What you see: 13 Oct: SPY NEGATIVE, spot 778.10, flip 779.8, put wall 755, call wall 800, net −0.94 bn, data 2026-10-12, inventory unavailable.

What it means: if the estimate's assumed dealer side is right, hedging could amplify moves below 779.8. But the sign is assumed and the data is a session old.

What a trader does: expects wider swings and sizes smaller. Uses 779.8, 755 and 800 as reference levels to watch, not as triggers. Checks whether price holds or loses the flip in the first hour.

Scenario: nothing new

What you see: for example, the measured score moves 54.3 to 54.8 and stays in the same band, the published score moves one point (83 to 84), events have no consensus forecasts, IC 5d near zero. (Partly from 13 Oct.)

What it means: the macro picture did not change overnight.

What a trader does: nothing on this tab. Keeps yesterday's macro read, spends the time on levels and triggers in the Next session plan.

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Read the measured index before the published one. Data first, opinion second.
  • Use the component table to name the stress: "it is semis and small caps" is more useful than "55".
  • Check "What followed" every time you feel macro fear. It often shows that the fear did not pay.
  • Open "Dated observations" and read every date.
  • Count echoes: the packet's probabilities copy the Colab models. One opinion repeated is still one opinion.
  • Always read the event report's counter-case.

Common mistakes

  • Reading an uncalibrated score (84, confidence 76, fragility 4.5) as a probability.
  • Mixing the published ladder (66-85) with the board's bands (60 and above).
  • Treating FRESH as "current": the content can be days old.
  • Reading "IV rank 100" as "options at a yearly high".
  • Treating a missing source as calm.
  • Trading the GEX flip as a signal when the dealer sign is assumed.
  • Reading a FRED "Change" on a rate as points instead of a % change.
