Scorecards

When each macro source made a call in the past, was it right, and how much evidence is there to judge it?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

Every macro source sounds confident. This tab checks them against what the market actually did. It grades four sources: the Colab daily regime model, the archived LLM macro packets, the board's own leans, and the published US Money Index.

It answers the question a professional asks before listening to anyone: "What is your record?" A source earns weight in your thinking only when its record is long enough to judge and shows it adds something beyond the market's normal drift.

Check it weekly, and any time you are tempted to act on a call from the Macro intelligence tab. It is the measuring stick for that tab.

Display onlyThe scorecards describe past calls. They never switch a source on or off, and they never feed a gate, score or rank. Most of the record on 13 Oct is too short to judge, and the tab says so with a "?".

What it does

For each source the board turns every past call into a direction, then measures what the market did after it.

  1. Find the entry session. A call counts from the first session it could have been traded. For the Colab regime model, a row dated D is computed from D's close, so the entry is the next session's open. For a packet or a Money Index revision, the entry is the first session whose regular open (13:30 UTC in the configuration) is at or after the time the packet was generated. For the board's leans, the entry is the session after the snapshot's session.
  2. Turn the call into a direction. Regime model: a regime containing "ON" is up (+1), "OFF" is down (−1). Packets: the label and risk switch are searched for BULL or RISK_ON (up) and BEAR or RISK_OFF (down); both or neither means no call. Board: UP or DOWN leans only. The Money Index gets no direction: it is grouped by its own band instead.
  3. Measure the return. From the entry session's open to the close 1, 5 or 20 sessions later (for 1 session, open to close of the same day). Scored on SPY and QQQ for the outside sources, on every ETF with current data for the board.
  4. Score it. The hit rate is the share of calls where the move went the called way. The signed return is the average return in the called direction: +1% means the calls made 1% on average if followed, −1% means they lost 1%.
  5. Count honest evidence. Calls a day apart share most of a 5- or 20-session window. The board thins them to independent windows (no overlap). Fewer than 8 independent windows gets a "?" and grey text.
  6. Latest wins. When several packets or revisions land before the same open, only the newest counts. Older ones are kept in the history as "superseded".

The tab is rebuilt on every board build from files on disk: the regime model CSV, the packet archive, the board's saved evening snapshots and the published Money Index's revision history. Each new session adds calls and completes older windows.

Why a base rate mattersMarkets rise more often than they fall. Over the board's history (since about August 2021) SPY rose on about 53% of next-day windows, 58% of 5-session windows and 64% of 20-session windows. A source that always says "bull" would score those hit rates without knowing anything. Always compare a hit rate with that base.

Reading the screen

1. Heading

The subtitle restates the rules: scored from the next tradable open, hit = direction matched the sign of the move, signed = return in the called direction, "?" = fewer than 8 independent windows.

2. Colab daily regime model (top left)

Read the line under the title first: how many calls, over what dates, and the model's own claimed accuracy. Then the table. Only trust cells without a "?".

3. LLM macro packets (top right)

Read the counts line: how many packets were generated, how many entry sessions they reduce to, how many made a directional call. Then the table. Open "Packet history" to see each packet and whether it counted.

4. This board's own leans (bottom left)

The board grades itself. Compare the "Hit" and "Signed" columns with the "Price-only" columns on the same row. The difference is what macro adds.

5. US Money Index by its own ladder band (bottom right)

No hit rates here, because the index makes no directional call. Instead, read the average move and up-rate of SPY and QQQ after the index sat in each band. Open "Revision history" to see each revision's score and band.

Scenarios

Scenario: a source with a real record that beats the base

What you see: for example, a source's 20d row shows hit 72%, signed +1.8%, 14 windows with no "?", while SPY's 20d base up-rate is about 64% and its base mean about +0.9%. (Illustrative values.)

What it means: enough independent evidence, and both numbers beat what a permanent bull would score.

What a trader does: gives that source's calls some weight as context on the Macro intelligence tab, still behind price and levels. Keeps watching: the record can fade.

Scenario: hit rate and signed return disagree (13 Oct)

What you see: the board's own 1d leans: hit 40% on 5 scored, signed +0.14%. The LLM packets on SPY 1d: hit 60%, signed +0.01%.

What it means: hit rate alone misleads. The board was wrong more often but the right calls were bigger. The packets were right more often but made almost nothing.

What a trader does: judges a source by signed return and window count first, hit rate second. Here neither has enough windows (5 calls; 15 windows for a one-day horizon) to say anything. No change in how much weight either gets.

Scenario: everything is thin or missing

What you see: 13 Oct: regime model 5d and 20d show dashes and "0 ?"; packet 5d and 20d are "3 ?" and "1 ?"; board 20d has 0 scored, 39 pending. For example, a missing archive would show "No scorable calls." (Illustrative.)

What it means: nobody has a judgeable record at the 5- and 20-session horizons yet.

What a trader does: treats every outside call as unproven context. Bases decisions on price, levels and the measured evidence on other tabs. Comes back weekly.

Scenario: a scary label with calm follow-through

What you see: Money Index band RISK OFF TRANSITION CONFIRMED: SPY 5d +0.56% with 75% up, QQQ 20d +4.46% with 100% up, but only 3 and 1 windows ("?").

What it means: so far, the published "risk-off" band has not been followed by declines. The sample is far too small to say it never will be.

What a trader does: does not short because the label says risk-off. Waits for price confirmation. Notes the result and checks again as windows accumulate.

Scenario: the packet in force changed before the open

What you see: in Packet history, 2026-10-12 21:10 ▼ bear, Entry 2026-10-13 · superseded (greyed); 2026-10-13 12:46 – none, Entry 2026-10-13, counted.

What it means: the evening bearish call was replaced by a neutral morning packet before the 13 Oct open. Only the neutral one is scored.

What a trader does: uses the packet that was current before the open, not the one read the night before. On Macro intelligence, always check the packet's as-of time.

Scenario: nothing to do

What you see: for example, after one more session the packets' 1d row moves from 15 to 16 windows and the hit rate from 60% to 59%. Nothing new crosses 8 windows. (Illustrative.)

What it means: the record did not change in any way that matters.

What a trader does: nothing. Closes the tab and moves to the Next session plan. A weekly look is enough.

Applying it to a trade

Coach: on the morning of 14 Oct the newest LLM packet says TRANSITIONAL BEARISH, and you hold a QQQ call idea for about 5 sessions. Should the bearish label change your plan? Let us ask the scorecard.

  1. Find the right card and horizon. LLM macro packets, QQQ row, 5d columns.
  2. Read the window count first. QQQ 5d: "3 ?". Three independent windows. Below 8, so it is grey.
  3. Then hit and signed. Hit 38%, signed −1.14%. If you had followed the packet's 5d calls on QQQ, you would have lost on average. But three windows is luck territory.
  4. Check the 1d record for context. QQQ 1d: 47% hit, −0.08% signed, 15 windows. Below QQQ's base up-rate of about 53%. No edge.
  5. Check this exact packet. Packet history: 2026-10-13 21:54, ▼ bear, PARTIAL (one copy said TRANSITIONAL only), Entry "pending". Its direction rests on part of the packet.
  6. Check the board itself. Board 5d: 3 scored, 33% hit, −1.53% signed; price-only 1 scored. The board has not earned weight either.
  7. Check the Money Index band. QQQ after RISK OFF TRANSITION CONFIRMED: 5d +0.34%, 63% up, "3 ?". Not bearish, also thin.
QQQ, 5-session idea, 14 Octpacket 5d: 38% hit · −1.14% · 3 windows ?

The bearish packet has no judgeable 5-session record, and its short-horizon record shows no edge over QQQ's normal drift. The Money Index band record is thin and not bearish.

Decision sentence: "The packet's bearish label is unproven, so it does not cancel my QQQ setup; I let my price trigger and the Next session plan decide, and I do not add size because of any macro call." This is a reading of the tab, not a recommendation.

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Read in this order: windows, signed, hit.
  • Compare every hit rate with the base: about 53% (1d), 58% (5d), 64% (20d) for SPY.
  • On the board card, the gap between macro leans and price-only leans is the only number that says whether macro helps.
  • Write down a source's record before you act on its call. It keeps you honest.
  • Revisit weekly; records change slowly.

Common mistakes

  • Trusting a self-reported accuracy (0.580, 0.39) over the board's own scoring.
  • Reading a "?" cell as evidence.
  • Calling 75% on four calls "a good model".
  • Treating the board's "Scored" count as independent windows: it pools many ETFs on the same days.
  • Mixing the published ladder bands here with the board's measured bands on Macro intelligence.
  • Reading "pending · superseded" on the newest packet as "replaced".
