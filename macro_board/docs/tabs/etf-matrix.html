ETF matrix

Across all 115 ETFs on the board, which ones have measured history pointing one way from here, how far has each already moved, and what does the option market charge for the next move?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

The ETF matrix is the board's screening table. It puts every ETF in the universe on one row: its recent returns, its trend, its volatility, its measured lean, what the AVSHUNTER pipeline proposes, and what options cost. An ETF (exchange-traded fund) is a basket of stocks, bonds or commodities that trades like one share.

The question it answers is narrow: "Given today's macro conditions, where has history been one-sided, and is the price and the option market lined up with it?" It does not tell you what to buy. It tells you where to look first.

Use it after the Daily review, Day story and Overview, once you know the state of the market. Use it before the Transmission map, Sector rotation and Desk card, which go deeper on the few ETFs you pick here. Click any row and a drawer opens with the full evidence for that ETF.

Display onlyNothing on this tab feeds back into the pipeline. The board reads AVSHUNTER data read-only, never re-ranks the pipeline and never gates a trade. A human reads it and decides.

What it does

Inputs

  • Daily prices for 115 ETFs in 10 groups (Broad indices, Factors, SPDR sectors, Industries & themes, Rates & credit, Commodities & dollar, International, Crypto, Volatility products, Leveraged & inverse). They come from the pipeline's price store, with gaps filled by the board from Polygon grouped daily bars.
  • Today's 10 macro conditions (SPY trend, realised volatility, breadth, net liquidity, curve level, curve move, 10Y yield, credit, dollar, oil). The Transmission map guide explains each one.
  • Option chains from MarketData.app, stored in the phantom history database (read-only).
  • tastytrade market metrics (implied volatility, IV rank, historical volatility, beta), fetched read-only. Stale after 30 hours.
  • Issuer holdings files for SPY, DIA, QQQ and the 11 SPDR sectors. Stale after 3 sessions.
  • The latest full AVSHUNTER run (on 13 Oct: run 20261013_221020), read-only.
  • The LLM macro packet's sector lead and avoid lists.

How a lean is measured

  1. Find analog sessions. An analog session is a past day whose 10 macro conditions looked like today's. The board starts by demanding a 100% match and loosens it (90%, 80%, 70%, 60%) until it has at least 12 independent 20-session windows. On 13 Oct it settled at 60% (6 of 10 conditions matching) with 129 analog sessions.
  2. Measure what followed. For every past session it takes the return from the next session's open to the close 1, 5 or 20 sessions later. That is what you could actually have traded after reading the board.
  3. Count independent windows. Back-to-back analog days share most of their future, so the board thins them: two 20-session windows only count separately if they start at least 20 sessions apart. This count is called independent windows (shown as "Windows").
  4. Split train and holdout. The last quarter of history is the holdout: kept aside to check the result. The first three quarters are the train period.
  5. Decide the lean on the train period. A lean of Up or Down needs at least 8 independent windows, a t of at least 1.5 and a mean move of at least 0.05% (1d), 0.25% (5d) or 0.75% (20d). Here t is the mean divided by its standard error: how many "noise units" the average sits away from zero.
  6. Check the holdout. If the holdout mean points the same way, the lean "agrees". With fewer than 3 holdout windows it is "thin".
  7. Add context. The board adds the move already made (in units of the ETF's own volatility), a response label, the option market's price for movement, tastytrade volatility metrics and holdings.

Every threshold above lives in macro_board/config/macro_board_config_v1.json and is marked provisional.

When it updates

Each board build refreshes the tab. The evening build after the close is the scored record. The morning build before the open shows the same closing session; on 14 Oct it also carried tastytrade metrics and holdings files fetched that morning. The example values here come from the morning build of 14 Oct, which shows the 13 Oct close.

Reading the screen

1. Title and controls

The title reads "ETF matrix" with a count, for example "115 shown · lean and evidence columns for the selected horizon". Below it sit four controls: the horizon buttons (1d, 5d, 20d), a group dropdown, a ticker filter and a "current data only" box. Pick the horizon first. It changes the lean, interval, response, priced-vs-history and evidence columns, and it carries over to the other tabs.

2. The table

Rows sit under grey group headers in the config's order. Read each row left to right in four blocks:

  • Identity and freshness: Ticker, Last, Data.
  • What price did: 1d, 5d, 20d, 60d returns, RS vs SPY 20d, vs 200d, from 1y high, RV20.
  • What history says: Lean, 80% interval, then Holdout, Analog mean, Base mean, Excess, Up %, Windows at the right.
  • What others say and what it costs: Pipeline, Response, Move made 20d, IV rank, Priced vs history, Packet.

Click a column header to sort. The first click sorts high to low, the next low to high. Sorting drops the group headers; a "Group view" button brings them back. Empty values always sort last.

3. The note under the table

It reminds you that "Short history" ETFs have fewer than 500 sessions, that leveraged, inverse and volatility products have their 5d and 20d leans withheld, and which pipeline run the Pipeline column reads.

4. The drawer (click a row)

A panel slides in from the right. Read it top to bottom: header line and price chart, what the pipeline proposes, measured lean by horizon, stage of the move, momentum history, what the option market charges, tastytrade volatility metrics, holdings (where a holdings file exists), response, and which conditions moved the ETF. Press Escape or "Close ✕" to shut it. "Desk card →" appears for the 56 ETFs that have a desk card and jumps there.

Scenarios

Scenario: everything agrees, but macro adds little (TLT, 20d)

What you see: TLT (20+ year Treasuries) shows ▼ Down with ✓ holdout. Interval -2.4% … -0.6%. Analog mean -1.49% against a base of -0.87%, so Excess -0.63%. Response Supporting, Not extended -1.0σ, Pipeline PUT · ARMED, priced vs history 1.01× with a 2.4% spread. In the drawer the price-only lean is also ▼ Down. The distance box shows only 1.37% to the pipeline target and 6.67% to its invalidation.

What it means: Everything points the same way. But TLT has drifted down in all conditions since 2020, so most of the lean is the ETF's own drift. Macro adds about 0.6 points.

What a trader does: Keeps TLT on the list, but does not treat it as a macro edge. The pipeline target is close and the invalidation is far, which is a poor shape. The pipeline itself flags IV percentile 88 (dear volatility). A trader waits for a better entry or sizes small, and checks the Desk card.

Scenario: a lean that failed its own test (XLC, 20d)

What you see: XLC shows ▲ Up but ✗ holdout. The train period averaged +1.66% (t 1.59, 21 windows) while the holdout averaged -1.47% (5 windows). Response Rejecting relief: a measured tailwind, yet XLC fell 1.53% over 20 sessions. The packet lists it as "lead". The pipeline is CALL · STAND DOWN because the spread is too wide; the board's own straddle spread is 110% of mid.

What it means: History before mid-2025 favoured XLC in conditions like these. The last year did not. The price is not responding to the tailwind either.

What a trader does: Skips or waits. A failed holdout plus a rejecting response outweighs the packet's opinion. Even if the view were right, the option spread makes it untradeable.

Scenario: short history and stale pieces (IJR, QQQ holdings)

What you see: IJR shows "Short history" in the Lean column (162 usable sessions, 500 needed). vs 200d is a dash and the drawer says "No option chain stored for IJR". In QQQ's drawer the holdings header reads "as of 2026-10-02" with a stale chip, 7 sessions old.

What it means: The board cannot measure IJR against macro history and has no option quotes for it. QQQ's contribution and participation numbers rest on a holdings file a week and a half old.

What a trader does: Treats IJR's evidence as unknown, never neutral, and reads it on price alone (or uses IWM for small caps). For QQQ, treats the holdings numbers as approximate and re-checks them once the Invesco file refreshes. If a row ever shows a STALE Data chip, the trader does not act on it at all.

Scenario: nothing to do (SPY, 20d)

What you see: SPY – No clear lean. Interval -0.5% … +1.4%. Analog +0.42% against base +0.92% (Excess -0.51%). Up 61% on 26 windows. Response Absorbing pressure. Not extended 1.0σ. IV rank 29, priced vs history 0.96×. Pipeline CALL · ARMED.

What it means: Sessions like today did slightly worse than average for SPY, but not distinguishably. The interval straddles zero. Options are fairly priced against history.

What a trader does: Takes no macro view on SPY. If acting on the pipeline's call, the trader judges it on its own evidence (Desk card, Next session plan) and does not claim macro support it does not have.

Scenario: a sharp fall that history says often rebounds (SMH)

What you see: SMH (semiconductors): 5d -7.02%, six down closes in a row, "Extended ↓ -1.9σ" over 5 sessions, yet 20d +8.49%. Lean No clear lean. Response 20d Absorbing pressure. Priced vs history 1.35× at 20d with a 15.8% spread; IV rank 26. Not in the pipeline run. The drawer's momentum table: after a 5d extended-down move, SMH's next 20 sessions averaged +5.39% (✓ holdout, 24 windows) against +2.70% for all sessions.

What it means: The pullback is large for SMH's own volatility. On SMH's own history, sharp 5-day drops were more often followed by gains. Options already price more movement than history delivered after similar macro days.

What a trader does: Does not assume the fall continues, and does not assume a bounce either. The rebound pattern is worth checking on the Transmission map (curve level is its strongest headwind) and Scenario watch. Options look dear, so a trader would size smaller or wait for IV to ease.

Scenario: a leveraged product (TQQQ)

What you see: TQQQ shows "Path-dependent" at 5d and 20d. The drawer shows a warning box. The evidence is still there: analog 20d mean +1.40%.

What it means: TQQQ resets its 3× leverage daily, so its multi-session return depends on the path, not just the start and end. History does not transfer mechanically.

What a trader does: Reads QQQ's row for the view and treats TQQQ only as an expression, with a short holding period in mind.

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Pick the horizon first. Everything right of "RV20" changes with it.
  • Sort by Excess, then read the top and bottom few in group view.
  • Hover every lean chip. Windows and the holdout matter more than the arrow.
  • Compare the lean with the price-only lean in the drawer. Same answer means drift, not macro.
  • Read the spread before the ratio. A cheap ratio at a wide spread is not cheap.
  • Check the Data column and the drawer's stale chips before anything else.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a lean as a probability or a signal to trade. It is recorded history.
  • Reading a 20d lean as a call on tomorrow.
  • Ignoring a ✗ holdout because the packet or pipeline agrees.
  • Reading "Extended" as "too late" without checking the momentum history.
  • Treating Response "Supporting" as permission. It describes behaviour only.
  • Reading leans on leveraged or volatility products.
  • Adding the condition bars together. They overlap and do not sum.
