Sector rotation

Which sectors are gaining or losing ground against SPY, where each sits in the rotation cycle, and has history in today's macro conditions favoured any of them relative to SPY?

Example values from the board as of the 13 Oct 2026 close

Purpose

Money moves between sectors. When investors leave technology for energy, energy funds outperform the market even if both fall. That movement is called sector rotation. This tab tracks it for the 11 SPDR sector ETFs (XLK, XLF, XLE and so on) and for 15 industry and theme ETFs (semiconductors, banks, biotech, homebuilders and others).

Everything here is relative to SPY. A sector that falls 1% while SPY falls 3% has outperformed by 2 points. That is a different question from "will it go up?", which belongs to the ETF matrix. A sector can lead the market while falling; the board calls that a defensive preference, not a buy call.

Use this tab after the ETF matrix and Transmission map, when you have a view and want to choose which sector fund expresses it, or to check whether a sector you like is gaining or losing ground. It also scores the LLM packet's past sector calls, so you can see how much weight they deserve.

Display onlyRotation labels, leans and packet calls are information for a human. None of them is a permission, a probability or an input to the pipeline.

What it does

Rotation coordinates

For each ETF the board divides its price by SPY's price every day. That ratio is the ETF's relative strength (RS): it rises when the ETF beats SPY. From it the board makes two numbers:

  • RS trend = 100 × today's ratio ÷ its average over the last 50 sessions. Above 100: the ETF is stronger against SPY than its recent norm.
  • RS momentum = 100 × today's RS trend ÷ the RS trend 10 sessions ago. Above 100: that relative strength is improving.

These place each ETF in one of four quadrants: Leading (both above 100), Weakening (trend above, momentum below), Lagging (both below), Improving (trend below, momentum above). Missing data gives MISSING, never a quadrant. This is a transparent approximation of a relative rotation graph, not the proprietary JdK RS-Ratio.

Relative evidence

The board uses the same analog sessions as the ETF matrix (on 13 Oct: 129 sessions at a 60% match) but measures the ETF's return minus SPY's return over the same window, from the next open. A relative lean needs at least 8 independent windows, |t| of at least 1.5, and a mean of at least 0.03% (1d), 0.15% (5d) or 0.5% (20d). Those minimums are smaller than for absolute leans because relative moves are smaller. The last quarter of history is held out to check the result.

Other pieces

  • Relative returns over 5, 20 and 60 sessions: ETF return minus SPY return.
  • Participation and leading holdings from issuer files, for the 11 SPDR sectors only.
  • Packet bias: the LLM macro packet's sector_bias_map, mapped from sector names to SPDR funds through config/sector_etf_map_v1.json.
  • Quadrant record and transitions, pooled across the 11 SPDR sectors.
  • Packet scorecard: each archived packet's leading-minus-lagging sector basket, scored from the next open.

Settings live in config rotation and are all provisional. The tab updates with every board build.

Reading the screen

1. Controls

Horizon buttons (1d, 5d, 20d) and a two-way switch: "SPDR sectors" (default) or "Industries & themes". The switch changes the map, the ranking, the table and the heatmap. The quadrant record, transitions and packet card always use the SPDR sectors.

2. Rotation map (left card)

A square chart. Horizontal axis: RS trend. Vertical axis: RS momentum. The crosshair at 100/100 splits four coloured zones. Each ETF has a large dot (today) and a tail of smaller dots (one per week, 8 points). Read the tail's direction: sectors usually travel clockwise, improving → leading → weakening → lagging.

3. Macro-conditional ranking (right card)

Horizontal bars ranking each ETF by its mean return relative to SPY after sessions like today, with a thin whisker for the 80% interval. The lean label sits under each ticker. Look for bars whose whisker stays on one side of the centre line.

4. Rotation table

One row per ETF with quadrant, coordinates, relative returns, participation, leading holdings, macro lean and packet view. Click a row to open the same drawer as the ETF matrix.

5. Macro impact on sectors

A heatmap like the Transmission map, but on returns relative to SPY.

6. Three cards at the bottom

"Does the quadrant predict the next H session(s)?", "Where quadrants go next" and "LLM packet sector calls". Read them once a week; they change slowly and tell you how much to trust the map and the packet.

How sectors move together (live) At the bottom of the tab the board measures, every build, how much the sectors move as one crowd and how each one moves with SPY:
  • Crowding: average correlation between the 11 sectors over the last 60 sessions, with its history since 2021. Investors are picking sectors at or below the 20th percentile; The crowd is moving as one at or above the 80th, usually on fear.
  • Sector correlation: switch between 250 sessions, 60 sessions and "relative to SPY". The relative view is the rotation seesaw: where money goes when it leaves a sector.
  • Relationships shifting now: pairs whose 60-session correlation moved 0.3 or more from their 250-session norm.
  • Each ETF against SPY: correlation, beta, character (hedge, defensive, partial, market-like, amplifier), what it did on SPY's worst and best days, and the outside asset most linked to its move against SPY.

Scenarios

Scenario: measured lean, packet and trend agree (XLRE, 20d)

What you see: XLRE: Macro lean ▼ Down ✓ holdout, -2.42% vs SPY (interval -3.27% … -1.57%), packet HEADWIND ✓ agrees. vs SPY 20d -5.6, 60d -12.4. Participation 15%. Only the map disagrees: Improving for 4 sessions, after Lagging 5 sessions ago, on a 5-day bounce of +1.9 against SPY.

What it means: History in conditions like today, the packet and the longer trend all point to real estate lagging SPY. The bounce is short. The quadrant record shows Improving sectors did no better than average at 20d (-0.32% against -0.26%), and 30% of Improving sectors were back in Lagging a week later.

What a trader does: Treats XLRE as the weakest sector to avoid for a long, or as a relative-weakness candidate. The board shows the price-only lean is also Down, so part of this is a long-running drift. A trader would check the ETF matrix for the absolute picture and the option spread before any trade.

Scenario: a relative lean that failed its holdout (XLC, 20d)

What you see: XLC is Lagging for 12 sessions, -4.3 against SPY over 20 sessions. Its macro lean is ▲ Up with an interval of +0.02% … +1.39%, but ✗ holdout: the train period averaged +1.26% (t 2.46), the holdout -1.97% on 5 windows. The packet calls XLC MIXED in its bias map yet lists Communication Services among its strongest sectors.

What it means: The older record liked XLC in these conditions; the last year did not. The packet is not consistent with itself, and the tape is weak.

What a trader does: Waits. A failed holdout and a lagging tape outweigh a mixed packet. A trader would want to see XLC move into Improving and the holdout evidence recover before taking a relative long view.

Scenario: a leader rolling over (XLK)

What you see: XLK is Weakening for 4 sessions; 5 sessions ago it was Leading. RS trend 102.2, momentum 97.5. vs SPY: -2.7 over 5 days but +4.1 over 20 and +6.9 over 60. Participation is still 72%. Packet HEADWIND, "unmeasured" because the macro lean is No clear lean.

What it means: Technology has led for months and has just started to give ground. On the transition record, a Weakening sector was back in Leading 25% of the time a week later, stayed 48% and slid to Lagging 25%.

What a trader does: Does not call a top from one quadrant change. A trader holding technology exposure watches the tail: a move into Lagging would confirm the rotation; a hook back up would not. The packet's headwind view stands alone here.

Scenario: missing and thin data (industries and the packet record)

What you see: Switch to "Industries & themes". Participation shows a dash for every row and "Leading holdings 20d" is empty: the board has issuer files only for SPY, DIA, QQQ and the SPDR sectors. Packet bias is a dash for all industries. In the packet card the 5d windows read "4 ?" and the 20d "1 ?".

What it means: For industries you have rotation and relative evidence only, not holdings breadth or a packet view. The packet's 5d and 20d record is too thin to judge.

What a trader does: Uses the parent sector's participation as a rough proxy (XLK's 72% for SMH), and says so in the notes. Gives the packet's multi-day sector calls little weight until the record grows.

Scenario: nothing to do (XLV)

What you see: XLV is Lagging for 1 session, sitting near the crosshair (trend 99.0, momentum 97.8). vs SPY +1.7 over 5 days, -1.5 over 20. Macro lean No clear lean, -0.33% with an interval of -1.28% … +0.62%. Packet NEUTRAL.

What it means: Health care is drifting around the centre of the map. Nothing in history, the packet or the tape separates it from SPY.

What a trader does: Leaves it. A dot near 100/100 can change quadrant on any small move, so a fresh "Lagging 1d" label means little.

Scenario: the interval is clear of zero, but there is no lean (SMH, 20d)

What you see: In Industries & themes, SMH shows +1.61% vs SPY with an interval of +0.34% … +2.87%, yet the lean reads – No clear lean. The tooltip shows the train period alone had t 1.02, below the 1.5 needed; the holdout averaged +4.40% on 5 windows.

What it means: The lean is decided on the older three quarters of history only. Here the older data was weak and the recent quarter strong, so the full-sample interval looks better than the rule allows.

What a trader does: Respects the rule. A strong recent quarter on 5 windows is a hint worth watching, not a measured lean. SMH is also Weakening on the map, so a trader waits.

Applying it to a trade

The LLM packet's rotation signal reads COMMODITY ROTATION, with Energy strongest. You want to know whether XLE is a sensible fund to express an energy view, and how much the board supports it.

  1. Map. SPDR sectors, any horizon. XLE is in Leading, the furthest dot to the upper right: RS trend 105.4, momentum 106.3. Its tail dipped into Lagging in late September (98.9 / 91.7 on 22 Sep) and has swung back. Leading for 4 sessions.
  2. Table. vs SPY: +5.55 over 5 days, -0.80 over 20, +11.14 over 60. Participation 91%: almost the whole fund trades above its 50-session average. Leading holdings MPC, VLO, XOM. Packet TAILWIND, "unmeasured".
  3. Ranking at 20d. XLE tops the sectors at +0.88% vs SPY, but the whisker runs from -1.00% to +2.75% and the label is No clear lean. History in conditions like today does not separate XLE from SPY.
  4. Heatmap. XLE's relative row is entirely faded at 20d. No single channel stands out.
  5. Quadrant record. At 20d, Leading sectors averaged +0.01% vs SPY against -0.26% for all sessions. On the transition table, Leading stayed Leading 59% of the time a week later and slipped to Weakening 26%.
  6. Packet record. 20 calls; lead beat lag 55% at 1d with a +0.13% mean spread. Only 4 independent windows at 5d.
  7. ETF matrix check. XLE closed at its 1-year high, 5d Extended ↑ 1.7σ. Pipeline CALL · ARMED. IV rank 66; 20d straddle priced 1.07× history with a 10.1% spread.

Tips and mistakes

Coach's tips

  • Read the tail's direction, not just the dot.
  • Check "days in quadrant": a 1-day label is noise; a 19-day run is a trend.
  • Use the transition table to keep the clockwise story honest.
  • Look for agreement between the map, the measured lean and the packet. Two of three agreeing is common; all three is worth noting.
  • Check participation behind a leader.
  • Check the packet's scorecard before you weigh its sector calls.

Common mistakes

  • Reading "Leading" as "going up". It means beating SPY.
  • Assuming Improving leads to Leading. On this record it did 20% of the time within a week.
  • Treating packet agreement as confirmation. The packet is LLM-written and uncalibrated.
  • Reading a negative quadrant mean as "sectors fall". SPY's mega-cap weight made most sectors lag.
  • Using a relative lean as a market-direction call.
  • Ignoring a ✗ holdout because the map looks good.
