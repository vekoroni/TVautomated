ETF Macro Board: how to read it and use it

A guide for a junior trader. It explains what each part of the board measures, how to turn it into a trading decision, and what the board will not do for you.

Board version macro-board-v1.0.0 · examples use the board as of the 5 Oct 2026 close and the pipeline run of 6 Oct 06:04 · revised 6 Oct 2026 (runs are measured, desk card added)

What the board is

The ETF Macro Board is one HTML page, rebuilt each day. It answers four questions about SPY, QQQ and 113 other ETFs:

  1. What is the macro backdrop right now? Ten measured conditions: trend, volatility, breadth, liquidity, the yield curve, yields, credit, the dollar and oil.
  2. What did each ETF do after past days that looked like today? That is the "measured lean", with an honest count of how much evidence sits behind it.
  3. Is the trade still worth taking at today's price? Distance left to the pipeline's target, how far the move has already run, and what the option market charges for the move.
  4. Where is money rotating, and is the macro view right? Sector rotation, holdings, and scorecards that grade every macro opinion against what actually happened.
The board is a reader, not a trader. It reads AVSHUNTER's data and the outside macro reports. It never places orders and never changes the pipeline. The pipeline does not read anything the board produces.

Why there are no buy/sell signals

This is the most common question, and it is deliberate. There are three reasons.

1. The trade signals come from the AVSHUNTER pipeline

The pipeline decides direction, setup, target, invalidation and contract. The board shows that proposal next to its own evidence: the Pipeline column in the ETF matrix, and the "What the pipeline proposes" section in each ETF's drawer. In the run of 6 Oct 06:04 the pipeline had SPY and QQQ CALL · ARMED, DIA PUT · ARMED, XLK and SMH CALL · ARMED, and IWM unresolved and standing down. Those are your candidate trades. The board's job is to tell you whether the backdrop, the history and the price still support them.

2. The board only gives a direction when history clearly supports one

A measured lean shows ▲ Up or ▼ Down only when three tests pass:

  • there were enough independent past windows;
  • the average move was large enough and consistent enough;
  • the direction also held on recent history that was kept aside (the "holdout").

On most days most ETFs show – No clear lean. That is a finding in its own right: today's macro mix gives no measurable edge on that ETF, so the trade has to stand on the pipeline's setup and the price. On 5 Oct the only leans that also passed the holdout were TLT down over 20 sessions, EWZ up and JETS up over 1 session. On the rotation tab, XLP, XLRE and XLB are down relative to SPY.

3. Macro informs, it does not trade

AVSHUNTER's rule is that macro opinion is shown and measured, never turned directly into a trade. A packet that says "risk-on" or a risk score of 75/100 is a story to check, not an order. The Scorecards tab shows how those stories have done. So far none has enough evidence to trust on its own.

"No clear lean" is not "no trade". It means macro history adds nothing beyond the ETF's normal drift today. Take or skip the trade on the pipeline's setup, the entry price and the option price. Do not invent a direction from the macro story.

Why everything relates to SPY

SPY (the S&P 500 ETF) is the board's benchmark, and it appears in five places.

WhereWhat SPY is used forWhy
Macro conditions"SPY trend" and "Realised volatility" are measured on SPYThe broad market is the backdrop every ETF trades against
Relative strength"RS vs SPY 20d" and the whole Sector rotation tab are measured against SPYA sector that rises less than SPY is losing money flow even while it goes up
Rotation and holdingsSectors are pieces of SPY; holdings show which names move itExplains why SPY moved: e.g. META, AMD, NVDA, MSFT and AAPL drove its last 20 sessions

Options checkSPY has the deepest, tightest option marketIts straddle is the most reliable read of what the market charges for movement

Practical meaning: if you trade a sector ETF, ask two questions. Is the sector going up? Is it going up faster than SPY? A long call on a sector that lags SPY often underperforms a plain SPY call.

Running the board

From PowerShell, in the repo folder:

cd C:\Users\ACKVerissimo\AVSHUNTER-Intelligence
.\Run-MacroBoard.ps1

This downloads the latest issuer holdings and tastytrade volatility metrics, rebuilds the page (about 1–2 minutes) and opens it in your browser. The page is saved at macro_board\output\etf_macro_board.html; you can reopen it at any time without rebuilding.

When to run it: before the US open, after the morning macro refresh has landed, and again after the close if you want the day's final picture. Running it every trading day matters: each run saves the day's leans, and the board grades them later. That record is the only real proof of whether the board helps.

The input strip

Below the banner is a row of input chips. Read it before anything else.

✓ Fresh

The source is current. Use the panels that depend on it.

! Stale

The source is older than its limit. Read those panels as background only. Hover to see its age.

× Missing

Not available. The board shows "missing" rather than a neutral value. Never fill the gap with a guess.

The morning routine (10 minutes)

  1. Inputs. Check the input strip. Note anything stale, such as QQQ holdings when Invesco blocks the download.
  2. Backdrop. On the Overview, read the 10 condition tiles and "What changed (last 10 sessions)". A change is often more useful than a level.
  3. Index cards. Read the SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA cards: the pipeline chip, how much of the target is used, the response label, the measured lean and what options charge.
  4. Rotation. On Sector rotation, see which sectors lead, which are turning up, and what sessions like today did to each sector against SPY.
  5. Candidates. Open the Desk card tab. Every pipeline candidate and index ETF has a card with the six desk questions already filled from the board. Work through each card and record your decision.
  6. Advisory. On Macro intelligence, read the event risk and news overlay for anything scheduled today. Treat them as risk to manage, not direction.
  7. Record. Write your decision and the reason before the open. Once a week, check the Scorecards.

Tab by tab

Overview

Shows

  • The four index cards (SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA)
  • The 10 macro condition tiles with history
  • What changed in the last 10 sessions
  • Analog depth and recent analog episodes
  • The LLM packet's headline view

How to read it

  • Each tile shows a state (e.g. RISING), the value, a history line and a coloured state strip. The date at top right is when the data was observed; "1 session old" is normal for FRED data.
  • Analog depth says how many past sessions matched today and how closely (5 Oct: 61 sessions at a 70% match). Fewer matches means weaker evidence.
  • Recent analog episodes list the last times the macro looked like today, and what SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA did next. Use them as a sense check.

On an index card, read the chips in this order:

  • Pipeline: the proposal, and how much of its target is already used.
  • Response: what the price is doing against the measured macro pressure.
  • Move made: whether the ETF has already run.
  • The lean table: history after sessions like today.
  • The option table: what a move costs.

The drawer holds the full story for one ETF:

  • the pipeline proposal, with distance left to its target and to its invalidation measured from today's close;
  • the lean by horizon, next to a price-only lean;
  • stage of the move;
  • the option straddle table;
  • tastytrade volatility metrics;
  • the holdings contribution, for SPY, QQQ, DIA and the sector ETFs;
  • which macro conditions have moved this ETF.
Leveraged, inverse and volatility products. TQQQ, SQQQ, UVXY, VXX and similar products reset daily or roll futures, so multi-day history does not carry over. Their 5d and 20d leans show "Path-dependent" on purpose. Use them only for short, deliberate trades.

Transmission map

A heatmap: ETFs down the side, the 10 macro conditions across the top. Each cell shows how much better (blue) or worse (red) the ETF did over the chosen horizon while that condition sat in today's state, compared with its other states. Faded cells are weak (|t| below 1) and should be ignored.

Use it to see which conditions actually move an ETF. For example: does XLF do worse when the curve is flat? It replaces textbook assumptions such as "rising yields always hurt tech" with what this market has done since 2021.

Do not add cells together. The conditions overlap (yields and the curve move together), so each cell is a separate comparison.

Sector rotation

Rotation map

Each sector is placed by its relative strength trend against SPY (left–right) and that trend's momentum (down–up). The tail shows the last 8 weeks. Sectors usually travel clockwise: improving → leading → weakening → lagging.

Macro-conditional ranking

Which sectors beat or trailed SPY over the next 1/5/20 sessions after days like today, with an 80% interval whisker. This is the macro's measured impact on sectors.

Rotation table

Quadrant and days in it, returns against SPY, participation (share of the sector's weight above its 50-day average), leading holdings, the measured lean, and whether the LLM packet's sector view agrees.

Evidence panels

Does the quadrant predict anything (compare each row with "All sessions"), where quadrants go next, and how the packets' leading-vs-lagging sector calls have scored.

What history says (5 Oct):

  • Leading sectors stayed level with SPY over the next 20 sessions. The other quadrants trailed by about 0.3%. That is a small edge.
  • Leaders and laggards stay in their quadrant 59% of the time a week later.
  • "Improving" became "leading" only 20% of the time.

So use rotation to tilt (which sector ETF to prefer for a view you already have), not as a stand-alone signal.

Participation matters most for sectors. A sector in the leading quadrant with low participation is being carried by a few names and is fragile. On 5 Oct XLK had 89% of its weight above trend; XLRE had 0%.

Desk card

One card per pipeline candidate (▲ call · ▼ put) and per index ETF. The horizon defaults to the pipeline's own, and you can switch it.

Checks strip

  • Macro lean and Momentum: does history point the same way as the proposal? Supports / conflicts / no evidence. The numbers always show, even when they are too thin for a lean.
  • Room left: reward left to the pipeline target against risk left to its invalidation, from today's close.
  • Option price: what a move costs against history, IV rank and spread.
  • Data and Event risk.

Six questions

  • What changed · what should it affect · what is the market doing · what the pipeline proposes · is the entry still worth taking · what would change my mind.
  • Below them: an evidence table (macro, price-only, momentum).
  • At the bottom: your decision (Act / Wait / Skip / Insufficient information), the reason and what would change your mind.

Use Download record to save the decision as JSON in macro_board/output/journal/. Drafts stay in your browser until you clear them. The checks describe evidence; they never decide for you.

When the card says "the target may be stale", measured continuation after similar runs is larger than the room left to the pipeline's target. Ask for a re-evaluated target. Don't chase the old one, and don't dismiss the trend because the old target is close.

Macro intelligence

The outside reports, as published. They include:

  • the LLM macro packet;
  • the US Money Index risk-off score and its triggers;
  • the economic event report;
  • the news overlay;
  • the VIX complex, dealer gamma, rates and credit, and the liquidity monitor;
  • the Colab models, FRED prints and threshold flags.

Every card shows its source file, its as-of time and its age.

  • Scores such as 75/100, confidence 90 or fragility 4.7/5 carry an uncalibrated tag. Someone chose them; nobody has tested them as probabilities. 75/100 does not mean a 75% chance of anything.
  • The Money Index card lists the date of each value inside it. A packet written today can contain a HYG price from 10 Sep. Always check the date of the number you use.
  • Dealer gamma is a model estimate with an assumed sign. The Money Index itself reports that true dealer positioning is unavailable.
  • When an event shows "no consensus · surprise not measurable", there was no forecast to compare with, so any "surprise" label is opinion.

Scorecards

The scorecards grade every source of macro opinion against what SPY and QQQ did next, starting from the next open:

  • the Colab regime model;
  • the archived LLM packets;
  • the Money Index, grouped by its own bands;
  • the board's own daily leans, against price-only leans.

A "?" next to the window count means fewer than 8 independent windows: too little to trust either way.

On 5 Oct the Colab regime model was right 57% of the time one day ahead, but wrong more often than right at 5 days. No source has earned trust yet. Check this tab weekly, and adjust how much weight you give each source as its record grows.

Method

Definitions, thresholds and limits. Read it once in full and come back when a number surprises you. Key limits:

  • the history covers about 5 years, since Aug 2021, which is a single rate cycle;
  • analog sets are small;
  • every threshold is provisional until the board's own scorecard has tested it.

Runs and trends: measure, don't assume

A common textbook rule says an ETF that has risen several days in a row, or has moved far in a short time, is "extended" and the entry is late. This market's own history does not support that rule. From Aug 2021 to Oct 2026, over the next 20 sessions:

After…SPYQQQ
All sessions (normal drift)+0.92% · up 63%+1.18% · up 62%
5+ up closes in a row+2.19% · up 84% · 16 windows+2.86% · up 73% · 22 windows
6+ of the last 7 days up+1.42% · up 74%+1.71% · up 65%

Runs have more often continued than stalled. These figures come from mostly a bull market and rest on 16–22 independent windows, so treat them as evidence, not law.

The board therefore measures runs per ETF. Every drawer and desk card has a Momentum history table: what this ETF did next after the same streak, the same 5-day move and the same 20-day move.

  • Continuation that beats normal drift: e.g. QQQ after 5+ up closes, or TLT after 10 down closes. The run is a reason to stay with the trend, not to wait.
  • Ordinary drift: e.g. SPY after 3 up closes did +0.66% against +0.92% normally. The run itself tells you nothing; decide on the setup and the room left.
  • No evidence either way: the window count is small or the interval spans zero. Don't let the run push you in either direction.

From board to decision: the desk card

For each candidate, answer these six questions in writing before you trade. The Desk card tab fills them in for you; the right column shows where each answer comes from.

QuestionWhere on the board
1. What changed?
The genuinely new information since yesterday.		Overview → What changed; Macro intelligence → event risk and news overlay (check observation dates)
2. What should it affect?
Which ETF, through which holdings.		Transmission map; drawer → holdings; Sector rotation → heatmap
3. What is the market doing with it?Response label; RS vs SPY; participation; Move made
4. What does the pipeline propose?Pipeline column; drawer → What the pipeline proposes
5. Is the entry still worth taking?Drawer → % left to target vs % to invalidation, share of the move already used; Priced vs history; IV rank

Then write one sentence: "Against [backdrop], [new information] affects [ETF] through [mechanism]. The market is [response]. The pipeline proposes [trade]. At [price] there is [room left] against [risk]. I will [act / wait / skip] because [reason]. I will change my mind if [condition]."

Rules and red flags

Do

  • Start from the pipeline's proposal; use the board to check it.
  • Measure room left from today's price, not the proposal price.
  • Compare the lean with the price-only lean. If they match, macro adds nothing.
  • Prefer options when "priced vs history" is near or below 1.
  • Check the observation date of any number you quote.
  • Write the decision and the reason before the open.

Don't

  • Treat 75/100, confidence 90 or fragility 4.7 as probabilities.
  • Buy because a lean says Up when its holdout says ✗.
  • Assume a run is late, or that it must continue. Check Momentum history.
  • Chase a pipeline target that is almost reached, unless the card says it may be stale and you have a re-evaluated target.
  • Read a "?" (thin evidence) as confirmation.
  • Use multi-day leans on leveraged or volatility products.
  • Add up heatmap cells or count two reports of the same event as two signals.
Red flags that mean "stop and ask"
  • An input chip is red on a panel you are relying on.
  • The pipeline and the measured lean point in opposite directions.
  • The option spread is above about 5% of the straddle price.
  • Breadth or participation is collapsing under a rising index.

Glossary

TermMeaning
Analog sessionsPast days whose macro conditions matched today's (at the stated match level).
Independent windowsThe number of non-overlapping periods behind a statistic. Twenty days in a row count as one 20-day window. This is the real sample size.
Measured lean▲ Up / ▼ Down when history clearly favours a direction; otherwise "No clear lean", "Insufficient sample" (too few windows), "Short history" (ETF too new in the database) or "Path-dependent" (leveraged or volatility product).
Holdout ✓ / ✗ / thinWhether the lean held on the last quarter of history, which was kept aside when the lean was set.
Price-only leanThe same test on all history, ignoring macro. Shows the ETF's normal drift.
80% intervalThe likely range of the true average. If it spans zero, the sign is uncertain.
Excess vs baseAnalog average minus all-history average: what today's macro adds.
ResponseSupporting: price agrees with the measured pressure. Absorbing pressure: measured headwinds, but the ETF rose. Rejecting relief: measured tailwinds, but it fell. Mixed: pressures balance. Insufficient: nothing measured.
Move made (σ)The recent return divided by the ETF's own normal move size (beyond ±1.5σ = extended). Whether runs like this continued is measured under Momentum history, never assumed.
Momentum historyWhat the ETF did next after the same streak, 5-day move and 20-day move, against its normal drift, with intervals and the holdout check.
Stale targetThe desk card's note when measured continuation after similar runs is larger than the room left to the pipeline's target, often a prior high.
Priced vs historyThe at-the-money straddle's expected move (from real bid/ask quotes) divided by the average move after similar sessions.
IV rank · IV − HVWhere implied volatility sits in its 1-year range (0–100) · implied minus realised volatility (positive = options above recent movement).
ParticipationShare of an ETF's weight in holdings trading above their 50-day average. Low participation under a rising price = narrow, fragile move.
Contribution (pp)A holding's start-of-window weight × its return, in percentage points of the ETF's move.
QuadrantsLeading, weakening, lagging, improving: a sector's relative trend against SPY and its momentum.
UncalibratedA score chosen by its author and never tested as a probability.
Fresh / Stale / MissingData age against its limit. Missing is shown as missing, never as neutral.
